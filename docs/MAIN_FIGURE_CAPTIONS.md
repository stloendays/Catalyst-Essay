## Figure 1 | Process and economic evaluation reverse the leading ammonia ranking

**a,** Propagation from the atomic descriptor (N* at an fcc(211) step, *E*_N) through microkinetics (turnover frequency at 673 K), catalyst inventory (bed volume ≤ 90 m³) and the synthesis loop (*T*, *P* and *T*_sep optimized for each catalyst) to catalyst-dependent cost. The reverse arrow denotes the backward-design calculation from economic parity to the required catalyst improvement. **b,** Ru, Os and Fe step sites with N* (illustrative fcc(211) models built from each metal's bulk nearest-neighbour distance), together with the descriptor, metal price and atomic and economic ranks. **c,** Model activity volcano at 673 K for the 15 metals; the shaded region marks Ru, Os and Fe. **d,** Minimum catalyst bed volume against *E*_N; only Ru, Os and Fe satisfy the 90 m³ limit. **e,** Metal price against activity: Ru is **72 times** more active than Fe and **6,732 times** more expensive. **f,** Atomic activity rank against economic rank for all 15 metals: the leading three reverse (**Spearman ρ = −0.50**) while the full screen retains **ρ = 0.929**. **g,** Rolling Top-*K* Spearman ρ for *K* = 3–15. **h,** Catalyst-dependent cost of the three feasible metals: Fe **15.292**, Ru **22.031** and Os **25.832** US dollars per tonne of NH₃.

## Figure 2 | Published laboratory leaders are often not the plant-cost leaders

**a,** The Adaptive Catalyst Screening Agent (ACSA) extracts catalytic data from publications, Supporting Information and databases with the location of every value, reproduces the frozen hand-built cases, takes every candidate through kinetics, process optimization and cost (a descriptor-only lower bound decides which candidates need the full optimization) and compares upstream and plant-cost leaderboards. The scientific models are unchanged. **b,** Methanol: share of the within-paper comparisons (same pressure, H₂/CO₂ ratio and space velocity; **83** comparisons, **44** papers) in which the paper's leader is not the plant-cost leader, by the metric that defines the paper leaderboard: space-time yield **33/83** (error bar: paper-cluster bootstrap 95% confidence interval, 24–55%), single-pass yield **31/83**, conversion **16/83**, methanol selectivity **63/83**; recycled CO converted to reverse-water-gas-shift equilibrium, entry-optimal purge. The bar marks space-time yield with recycled CO treated as inert (**46/83**). **c,** Ammonia: the **124** within-paper comparisons (same temperature, pressure, H₂/N₂ ratio and space velocity; **28** papers) of the primary ammonia-synthesis papers. Left: the catalyst with the highest rate per gram of catalyst is not the plant-cost leader in **44** (error bars: paper-cluster bootstrap 95% confidence intervals; 18–52%), the catalyst with the highest rate per gram of metal in **54**, and with 90% Ru recovery in **43**. Right: what the 44 disagreements differ by, with the number of papers and the median regret: a commercial fused-iron reference is the plant-cost leader in **17** (3 papers; 29%); a catalyst of another metal in **14** (3 papers; 17%), an Fe catalyst on the same support in 12 of them; the same metal and content on another support or with another promoter in **11** (5 papers; 2%); the same metal at another content in **2** (2 papers; 0.5%). **d,e,** Two methanol comparisons: space-time yield against net production cost, with the space-time-yield leader (red) and the plant-cost leader (green). Re/TiO₂ at 100 bar (marker area scales with CH₄ selectivity) and K- or Ba-promoted Cu/Al₂O₃ at 10 MPa.[65] **f,** An ammonia comparison: rate per gram of catalyst against plant cost for Ru, Fe and Co on BaTiO₃₋ₓHₓ at 5 MPa;[54] the rate leader Ru/BaTiO₂.₅H₀.₅ (2.5 wt% Ru, red) and the plant-cost leader, Fe (1 wt%, green; regret 23%).

## Figure 3 | Metal price and process optimization jointly determine the Fe-Ru ranking

**a,** NH₃ synthesis loop with the cost components associated with each unit and the decision variables *T*, *P* and *T*_sep. **b,** Catalyst beds at each optimum, drawn to scale (converter L/D = 3; pellet size illustrative): Fe **17.1 m³** at 425 °C, 180 bar and *T*_sep = 30 °C; Ru **0.066 m³** at 450 °C, 425 bar and 25 °C; Ru priced as Fe **9.4 m³** at 425 °C, 170 bar and 30 °C. The Ru bed is 258 times smaller than the Fe bed but carries 26 times its metal-inventory cost. **c,** Lowest feasible cost against synthesis pressure with *T* and *T*_sep reoptimized: Fe 15.29 at 180 bar, Ru 22.03 and Os 25.83 at 425 bar; dashed line, Ru priced as Fe, 14.71 at 170 bar. **d,** Ru at its actual catalyst cost. Top, Ru reoptimized over all **14,136** process states against the effective metal price *p*(1 − *r*)/*u*, where *u* is the gain in exposed metal of a supported catalyst over fused iron and *r* the recovered fraction. Pure Ru in the common fused-iron formulation costs **22.03** US dollars per tonne; a promoted Ru/C catalyst (*u* ≥ 11: 11% Ru dispersion against < 1% exposed Fe) costs **17.95**; with 90–94% recovery it costs **15.67–15.49** at 195–200 bar, 0.38–0.19 above Fe. Ru matches Fe at an effective price of **164 US dollars per kilogram**. Diamonds, the same reading in the KAAP loop (90 bar, *T*_sep −20 °C): **18.53–18.91** against Fe in that loop (**19.07**, solid line). Squares, the Ru/C catalyst's own bed (3.2 wt% Ru; markers at 1,000 kg m⁻³, bars to 500 kg m⁻³) with 90–94% recovery: **15.89–16.44** in the main loop and **18.92–19.32** in the KAAP loop. Bottom, the Ru activity multiple needed for parity at each effective price, with full process reoptimization: **201×** for pure Ru, **18×** for Ru/C and **1.5–2.2×** with recovery. Shaded, effective prices at which a scaling-consistent Ru descriptor reaches Fe (≤ **237** US dollars per kilogram). **e,** Contribution of each cost component to the baseline **6.739** US dollars per tonne Ru − Fe difference, and the equal-price calculation: Ru at 8 US dollars per kilogram, fully reoptimized, costs **14.712**, or **0.580** below Fe. **f,** The 1,000 descriptor-uncertainty samples in (Fe *E*_N, Ru *E*_N), coloured by economic winner (Fe 681, Ru 319); dashed lines bound the Fe *E*_N interval that satisfies the 90 m³ bed limit. **g,** Outcomes across these samples: Fe feasible **79.9%**, Fe economic Top-1 **68.1%**, atomistic Top-1 equal to economic Top-1 **28.2%**, and Fe in the Top-3 **94.0%**. **h,** Ru − Fe cost difference across the **5,000** joint economic-uncertainty samples: Fe is less costly in **5,000/5,000**, with a minimum difference of **2.38** and a median of **7.11** US dollars per tonne.

## Figure 4 | Backward design separates economic targets from physically reachable catalyst-property regions

**a,** Activity-only backward design. Ru intrinsic activity is multiplied by \(\alpha\), and the full ammonia process is reoptimized at every value. The baseline Ru activity multiplier required to reach the Fe optimum is **201.22×**. The shaded red band shows the 5th–95th percentile range of the corresponding parity target over 5,000 joint economic-uncertainty samples (**70.78×–462.00×**); the green band marks the much smaller strict-scaling activity headroom. Bottom, measured activity gains of modified Ru over a reference Ru catalyst on the same axis: Ba promotion of Ru on carbon (**75×**, turnover frequency per surface Ru), Ba–Ru/BN at 5 MPa (**>100×**) and Cs–Ru/MgO (**>30×** at 300 °C, **>134×** at 350 °C) reach the parity band, and Cs promotion of Ru on carbon (**65×**) lies just below it; Cs promotion of Ru/YSZ (**10×**, per gram of Ru), electride and oxyhydride supports give **~10×** (Ru/C12A7:e⁻ 9.8×, Ru/BaTiO₂.₅H₀.₅ 8.4× at 5 MPa), Ru/Ba–Ca(NH₂)₂ **≥33.5×** (\*against Cs–Ru/MgO), and Ru confined inside carbon nanotubes **0.5×**. Circles, turnover frequency per surface site; squares, rate per mass at matched Ru content; arrows, lower bounds. **b,** Activity-only physical reachability along the strict \(E_N\) scaling relation. The minimum Ru cost is **21.398 US dollars per tonne of NH₃** at \(E_N=-1.215\) eV, remaining **6.106 US dollars per tonne** above Fe; the 673 K activity headroom is **1.090×**, and the largest state-specific gain over the process library is **2.525×**. **c,** Joint backward target region when direct Ru activity, catalyst lifetime and Ru recovery are allowed to improve together. Curves give the Ru recovery required for Fe parity for 10-, 15- and 20-year catalyst lifetimes. At 99% recovery, the conservative upper bound on the required direct activity multiplier falls to **2.418×**, **1.742×** and **1.401×**, respectively. These curves define economic targets and are not used as evidence of physical reachability. **d,** Exact strict-scaling × lifecycle reachability over all **14,136** process states. The tested property box (catalyst lifetime ≤20 years and Ru recovery ≤99%) remains outside the parity region: its best corner, 20 years and 99% recovery, gives **15.3625 US dollars per tonne of NH₃**, **0.0708 US dollars per tonne (0.46%)** above Fe. Parity lies just outside the box, requiring **99.1186% recovery at 20 years** or **22.69 years of catalyst lifetime at 99% recovery**. The strict-scaling parity state occurs at approximately \(E_N=-1.230\) eV, 425 °C, 190 bar and a 30 °C separator.


## Figure 5 | Selectivity, recycle and purge reshape the methanol ranking

**a,** CO₂-to-methanol loop with a purge split. Methane formed as a side product accumulates in the loop and leaves through the purge, so CH₄ selectivity changes the loop composition as well as H₂ consumption, recycle and compression. **b,** Re/TiO₂ at 1 and 5 wt% Re (schematic; Re atoms shown in a 1:5 ratio on the same area). The four catalyst states were measured at 200 and 250 °C, 100 bar and CO₂:H₂ = 1:4.[13] **c,** The four states from measured catalyst properties (space-time yield per gram of Re, CO₂ conversion and CH₄ selectivity) through the process model at 2% purge to net production cost: **943, 962, 967 and 1,258** euros per tonne, shown in order of increasing cost. **d,** Rank probability across 5,000 draws of each state's own measurement uncertainty (CO₂ conversion and selectivities; full plant model re-solved in every draw): the economic winner keeps first place in **4,559/5,000** draws, 5 wt% Re at 250 °C stays last in **5,000/5,000**, and the second and third places exchange in **1,054/5,000**. **e,** Space-time-yield rank against net-cost rank: **ρ = 0.40**, **τ = 0.33**, with two of six pairwise comparisons inverted; the state ranked third by space-time yield per gram of Re is first economically. **f,** Net production cost over **396** purge levels from 0.5 to 40% and the Spearman ρ at each level; ρ never exceeds **0.80**. **g,** Local economic leverage at 5 wt% Re and 250 °C: CH₄ suppression **0.37579**, single-pass conversion **0.05883**, space-time yield **0.00289**.

## Figure 6 | Catalyst effects on process operation determine whether rankings change

**a,** Routes by which catalyst properties affect each reaction-specific economic objective. NH₃: metal price and intrinsic activity determine catalyst inventory and preferred operating conditions, which alter compression, reactor and equipment costs. Methanol: CH₄ selectivity, conversion and space-time yield affect loop composition, recycle, purge losses and catalyst requirement. Absolute costs are not compared across reactions. **b,** Au/TiO₂ CO-oxidation control: 2–6 nm Au particles on the same support, drawn to scale (illustrative particle shapes). **c,** Activity rank against catalyst-mass rank: no crossings, **ρ = τ = 1.000**, with the complete order preserved in **10,000/10,000** parameter samples drawn from ranges reported in the literature; 6 nm particles require **8.064 times** as much catalyst as 2 nm particles. **d,** Mass activity and required catalyst mass against Au diameter. **e,** Additional analysis allowing mild, moderate and strong candidate-specific kinetic and operating variation over 273–293 K and 273–313 K (moderate variation, 273–293 K: **92.16%** exact rank preservation). **f,** Spearman ρ before and after process propagation: NH₃, 0.929 across all 15 metals and −0.50 for the leading three; methanol, 0.40 at 2% purge (−0.6 to 0.8 over 0.5–40% purge); Au control, 1.000, with mean ρ = 0.992 under moderate candidate-specific variation.

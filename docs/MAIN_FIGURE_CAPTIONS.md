## Figure 1 | Process and economic evaluation reverse the leading ammonia ranking

**a,** Propagation from the atomic descriptor (N* at an fcc(211) step, *E*_N) through microkinetics (turnover frequency at 673 K), catalyst inventory (bed volume ≤ 90 m³) and the synthesis loop (*T*, *P* and *T*_sep optimized for each catalyst) to catalyst-dependent cost. The reverse arrow denotes the backward-design calculation from economic parity to the required catalyst improvement. **b,** Ru, Os and Fe step sites with N* (illustrative fcc(211) models built from each metal's bulk nearest-neighbour distance), together with the descriptor, metal price and atomic and economic ranks. **c,** Model activity volcano at 673 K for the 15 metals; the shaded region marks Ru, Os and Fe. **d,** Minimum catalyst bed volume against *E*_N; only Ru, Os and Fe satisfy the 90 m³ limit. **e,** Metal price against activity: Ru is **72 times** more active than Fe and **6,732 times** more expensive. **f,** Atomic activity rank against economic rank for all 15 metals: the leading three reverse (**Spearman ρ = −0.50**) while the full screen retains **ρ = 0.929**. **g,** Rolling Top-*K* Spearman ρ for *K* = 3–15. **h,** Catalyst-dependent cost of the three feasible metals: Fe **15.292**, Ru **22.031** and Os **25.832** US dollars per tonne of NH₃.

## Figure 2 | Metal price and process optimization jointly determine the Fe-Ru ranking

**a,** NH₃ synthesis loop with the cost components associated with each unit and the decision variables *T*, *P* and *T*_sep. **b,** Catalyst beds at each optimum, drawn to scale (converter L/D = 3; pellet size illustrative): Fe **17.1 m³** at 425 °C, 180 bar and *T*_sep = 30 °C; Ru **0.066 m³** at 450 °C, 425 bar and 25 °C; Ru priced as Fe **9.4 m³** at 425 °C, 170 bar and 30 °C. The Ru bed is 258 times smaller than the Fe bed but carries 26 times its metal-inventory cost. **c,** Lowest feasible cost against synthesis pressure with *T* and *T*_sep reoptimized: Fe 15.29 at 180 bar, Ru 22.03 and Os 25.83 at 425 bar; dashed line, Ru priced as Fe, 14.71 at 170 bar. **d,** Ru reoptimized over all **14,136** process states as its metal price varies from 1 to 3 × 10⁵ US dollars per kilogram. Ru matches the Fe optimum at **164 US dollars per kilogram**, and its optimal pressure rises from 170 to 420–430 bar as the metal becomes more expensive. **e,** Contribution of each cost component to the baseline **6.739** US dollars per tonne Ru − Fe difference, and the equal-price calculation: Ru at 8 US dollars per kilogram, fully reoptimized, costs **14.712**, or **0.580** below Fe. **f,** The 1,000 descriptor-uncertainty samples in (Fe *E*_N, Ru *E*_N), coloured by economic winner (Fe 681, Ru 319); dashed lines bound the Fe *E*_N interval that satisfies the 90 m³ bed limit. **g,** Outcomes across these samples: Fe feasible **79.9%**, Fe economic Top-1 **68.1%**, atomistic Top-1 equal to economic Top-1 **28.2%**, and Fe in the Top-3 **94.0%**. **h,** Ru − Fe cost difference across the **5,000** joint economic-uncertainty samples: Fe is less costly in **5,000/5,000**, with a minimum difference of **2.38** and a median of **7.11** US dollars per tonne.

## Figure 3 | Backward design shows that the Ru activity required for parity is outside the accessible range

**a,** Ru intrinsic activity is multiplied by α, the full process is reoptimized at every α, and α* is the multiplier at which Ru reaches the Fe optimum (15.292 US dollars per tonne). **b,** Required and attainable activity on the same axis: the α* distribution across the 5,000 joint economic-uncertainty samples (**5th percentile 70.8**, **95th percentile 462**) with the baseline **α* = 201.2**, compared with the scaling-relation maximum at 673 K (**1.09×**), the direct DFT check (**1.84×**) and the largest increase over all process states (**2.52×**). The 5th-percentile requirement is **28 times** larger than the attainable maximum. Top axis, the same multiplier expressed as *k*_B*T* ln α at 673 K. **c,** Reoptimized Ru cost against α and the corresponding optimal pressure; at α* Ru operates at 425 °C, 190 bar and *T*_sep = 30 °C. **d,** Activity increase relative to the current Ru state along the strict scaling relation at 673 K, compared with the required range. **e,** Lowest feasible Ru cost along the scaling relation: **21.40** US dollars per tonne at *E*_N = −1.215 eV, **6.11** above Fe.

## Figure 4 | Selectivity, recycle and purge reshape the methanol ranking

**a,** CO₂-to-methanol loop with a purge split. Methane formed as a side product accumulates in the loop and leaves through the purge, so CH₄ selectivity changes the loop composition as well as H₂ consumption, recycle and compression. **b,** Re/TiO₂ at 1 and 5 wt% Re (schematic; Re atoms shown in a 1:5 ratio on the same area). The four catalyst states were measured at 200 and 250 °C, 100 bar and CO₂:H₂ = 1:4.[13] **c,** The four states from measured catalyst properties (space-time yield per gram of Re, CO₂ conversion and CH₄ selectivity) through the process model at 2% purge to net production cost: **943, 967, 975 and 1,258** euros per tonne, shown in order of increasing cost. **d,** Rank probability across the 5,000 economic-parameter samples: every state retains the same rank in **5,000/5,000** samples for the reference cost model and for the additional active-Re replacement calculation. **e,** Space-time-yield rank against net-cost rank: **ρ = 0.20**, **τ = 0.00**, with three of six pairwise comparisons inverted; the state ranked third by space-time yield per gram of Re is first economically. **f,** Net production cost over **396** purge levels from 0.5 to 40% and the Spearman ρ at each level; ρ never exceeds **0.40**. **g,** Local economic leverage at 5 wt% Re and 250 °C: CH₄ suppression **0.37579**, single-pass conversion **0.05883**, space-time yield **0.00289**.

## Figure 5 | Catalyst effects on process operation determine whether rankings change

**a,** Routes by which catalyst properties affect each reaction-specific economic objective. NH₃: metal price and intrinsic activity determine catalyst inventory and preferred operating conditions, which alter compression, reactor and equipment costs. Methanol: CH₄ selectivity, conversion and space-time yield affect loop composition, recycle, purge losses and catalyst requirement. Absolute costs are not compared across reactions. **b,** Au/TiO₂ CO-oxidation control: 2–6 nm Au particles on the same support, drawn to scale (illustrative particle shapes). **c,** Activity rank against catalyst-mass rank: no crossings, **ρ = τ = 1.000**, with the complete order preserved in **10,000/10,000** parameter samples drawn from ranges reported in the literature; 6 nm particles require **8.064 times** as much catalyst as 2 nm particles. **d,** Mass activity and required catalyst mass against Au diameter. **e,** Additional analysis allowing mild, moderate and strong candidate-specific kinetic and operating variation over 273–293 K and 273–313 K (moderate variation, 273–293 K: **92.16%** exact rank preservation). **f,** Spearman ρ before and after process propagation: NH₃, 0.929 across all 15 metals and −0.50 for the leading three; methanol, 0.20 at 2% purge (−0.6 to 0.4 over 0.5–40% purge); Au control, 1.000, with mean ρ = 0.992 under moderate candidate-specific variation.

## Figure 6 | Adaptive calculation selection makes the multiscale analysis repeatedly executable

**a,** The Adaptive Catalyst Screening Agent (ACSA) selects among predefined scientific calculations under a finite compute-unit budget (CU; 1 CU is the wall time of 1,000 microkinetic state evaluations). Deterministic tools perform all kinetic, process and economic calculations. A run is counted as correct only when it identifies the economic winner, Fe-Ru ordering and reachability classification. **b,** Three calculation sequences that recover the same selection and reachability conclusions: the minimum sequence containing all scientific steps used in the analysis (**22 CU**); one strong-model run at a 150-CU allowance that builds 1,302- and 1,784-state process windows, holds the correct winner from 65 CU and satisfies all three outcomes at **102 CU**; and the fixed sequence, which evaluates the full 14,136-state window and recovers all three conclusions at **206 CU**. **c,** Recovery of all three conclusions against compute allowance for the strong, mini and nano model tiers, with a Wilson 95% interval for the strong model (*n* = 20 per cell, except 200 CU, *n* = 8, and 250 CU, *n* = 9); under the non-binding 5,000-CU allowance the strong model succeeds in 20/20 runs. **d,** Strong-model runs that use a reduced process window and the median smallest window used; under the non-binding allowance, 0/20 use a reduced window, showing that budget pressure activates search compression. **e,** Median computational cost at which all three conclusions become stable and median final cost against allowance, with the 22-CU minimum reference sequence and the 206-CU fixed sequence; under the non-binding allowance, **566** and **714** CU. **f,** Computational cost relative to the 22-CU reference: median stable cost in the 75-CU cell **2.39×**, 75-CU allowance **3.41×**, fixed sequence **9.36×**, and non-binding stable median **25.73×**. The comparison characterizes the budget-dependent allocation mechanism; the Agent remains the execution layer that orchestrates the deterministic calculations across repeated screening tasks. **g,** Action errors and empty turns by source at 175 CU (20 runs per cell), including the mini model with a structured tool interface.

## Figure 2 | Laboratory leaders are often not the plant-cost leaders

**a,** The Adaptive Catalyst Screening Agent (ACSA) reads 90 methanol and 52 ammonia papers with their Supporting Information: a language model extracts each value with its unit and page, the hand-built cases are reproduced first, and a lower bound on cost decides which candidates need the full optimization. Each candidate is then costed from the active site through the particle, pellet and multibed converter to the synthesis loop, and the laboratory leader is compared with the plant-cost leader. **b,** Methanol: share of comparisons within papers in which the leader by space-time yield (STY) is not the plant choice, between catalysts (more catalyst loaded, or at laboratory conversion) and over every operating point (with STY, conversion or selectivity as the leaderboard). Filled circles, CO, CH₄ and N₂ held to at most 6.86% of the reactor inlet, with 95% confidence intervals from a paper-cluster bootstrap. Bands, inlet limits of 5–10%. Open circles, no limit. **c,** Ammonia: the same share by rate basis (left) and the 12 disagreements by what separates the two leaders (right; one square per comparison; dot, cost gap above 5%). **d,** **e,** STY against net production cost for Re/TiO₂ at 100 bar (**d**; marker area, CH₄ selectivity) and InNi₃C₀.₅ on four supports at 300 °C and 4 MPa (**e**; marker area, CO selectivity), with the 6.86% limit (filled) and without it (open). **f,** Rate against plant cost for Ru, Fe and Co on BaTiO₃₋ₓHₓ at 5 MPa.[46] Red ring, laboratory leader. Green ring, plant choice.

## Figure 4 | Ru matches iron only at a 99% lower price

**a,** NH₃ synthesis loop with the cost components of each unit and the decision variables *T*, *P* and *T*_sep. **b,** Catalyst beds at the Fe, Ru and Ru-priced-as-Fe optima, drawn to scale. **c,** Lowest cost against synthesis pressure, with *T* and *T*_sep reoptimized. Shaded, prices at which a scaling-consistent Ru descriptor reaches Fe. **e,** Contributions of the cost components to the Ru − Fe difference and the equal-price calculation. **f,** Descriptor-uncertainty samples coloured by economic winner. Dashed, the Fe descriptor interval that meets the 90 m³ limit. **g,** Decision outcomes across these samples. **h,** Ru − Fe cost difference across 5,000 economic-uncertainty samples. Descriptor kinetics, costs in US dollars per tonne of NH₃.

## Figure 5 | With measured rate laws, supported Ru/C matches fused iron and the optimizer places it downstream

**a,** Ru/C − Fe catalyst-dependent cost against the Ru financing rate in a loop of the Kellogg Advanced Ammonia Process (KAAP) type at 90 bar and −20 °C (solid) and with each catalyst at its own optimum (dashed). Shaded, 32 disclosed catalyst-metal lease rates. Red, their median, the reference rate. **b,** Cost of the best design of each catalyst arrangement in a three-bed adiabatic converter at 9 MPa. Green, rates at which fused iron first and Ru/C downstream is optimal. All-Ru/C matches all-Fe at 2.46% per year rather than the 4.57% of **a**, because each adiabatic Ru/C bed must leave at 460 °C or below (4.32% and 4.96% with the limit at 500 °C and 530 °C). **c,** The least-cost converter at the reference rate, beds drawn to scale with their volume and separated by interbed coolers, with the temperature range and outlet NH₃ of each bed. **d,** Ratio of the Ru/C to the fused-iron rate per kilogram and per cubic metre of bed at 90 bar and 400 °C against the NH₃ mole fraction. Fused iron, Keestra et al. Model 3 (fitted at 1–324 bar). Ru/C (8 wt% Ru, 490 kg m⁻³, 95.55% recovery), Rossetti et al. (50–100 bar). Each catalyst is optimized inside its fitted pressure range.

## Figure 6 | Backward design separates economic targets from reachable catalyst properties

**a,** Ru activity multiple needed for parity with Fe, with the process reoptimized at each value. Red band, 5th–95th percentile under economic uncertainty. Green band, gain allowed by the strict scaling relation. Bottom, measured activity gains of modified Ru over a reference Ru catalyst, including Ru/Ba–Ca(NH₂)₂ against Cs–Ru/MgO[56] and Cs–Ru/YSZ[57] (circles, turnover frequency per surface site; squares, rate per mass at matched Ru content; arrows, lower bounds). **b,** Ru cost along the strict *E*_N scaling relation. **c,** Ru recovery required for parity at catalyst lifetimes of 10, 15 and 20 years when activity, lifetime and recovery improve together. **d,** Lowest strict-scaling cost over catalyst lifetime and Ru recovery across all 14,510 process states, with the parity boundary.

## Figure 1 | Process and economic evaluation reverse the leading ammonia ranking

**a,** Atomic, laboratory and plant-level optima along the forward propagation. The atomic optimum is set by the descriptor and intrinsic turnover (N* at an fcc(211) step, *E*_N; turnover frequency at 673 K), the laboratory optimum by measured activity or space-time yield at the test conditions, and the plant-level optimum is the plant-cost leader after catalyst inventory (bed volume ≤ 90 m³), the synthesis loop (*T*, *P* and *T*_sep optimized for each catalyst) and catalyst-dependent cost. Bars mark the segment of the chain that each case measures: ammonia from the atomic to the plant-level optimum (Figs 1, 4, 5 and 6); CO₂-to-methanol from published laboratory conversion, selectivity and space-time yield through recycle, purge and cost to the plant-level optimum (Figs 2b,d,e and 3); and the Au/TiO₂ CO-oxidation control from measured, size-dependent activity to the required catalyst mass, where the ranking is preserved (Extended Data Fig. 8). The ammonia literature comparisons of Fig. 2c,f use the same laboratory-to-plant segment as methanol. The reverse arrow denotes the backward-design calculation from economic parity to the required catalyst improvement. **b,** Ru, Os and Fe step sites with N* (illustrative fcc(211) models built from each metal's bulk nearest-neighbour distance), together with the descriptor, metal price and atomic and economic ranks. **c,** Model activity volcano at 673 K for the 15 metals; the shaded region marks Ru, Os and Fe. **d,** Minimum catalyst bed volume against *E*_N; only Ru, Os and Fe satisfy the 90 m³ limit. **e,** Metal price against activity: Ru is **607 times** more active than Fe and **6,732 times** more expensive. **f,** Atomic activity rank against economic rank for all 15 metals: the leading three reverse (**Spearman ρ = −0.50**) while the full screen retains **ρ = 0.911**. **g,** Rolling Top-*K* Spearman ρ for *K* = 3–15. **h,** Catalyst-dependent cost of the three feasible metals: Fe **11.516**, Ru **16.633** and Os **19.747** US dollars per tonne of NH₃.

First two bars: catalyst against catalyst, each at its best measured operating point (catalyst identity fixed before counting; Methods). With each entry free to load more catalyst and raise its conversion to 95% of equilibrium (cat., X raised), the space-time-yield leader is not the plant-cost leader in **21** of the **63** comparisons with at least two catalysts (error bar: paper-cluster bootstrap 95% confidence interval, 22–46%; hatched, 10 with a cost gap above 5%); at laboratory conversion (cat., lab. X), in **26** of **63** (29–53%; 19 above 5%). The other bars count every operating point of every catalyst at laboratory conversion, by the metric that defines the paper leaderboard: space-time yield **50/81** (49–72%), conversion **54/81**, methanol selectivity **30/81** (hatched, 42, 49 and 19 above 5%; single-pass yield, not shown, gives 50/81 with 42 above 5%); without the inlet limit, space-time yield **25/81** (17–45%; 10 above 5%). ## Figure 3 | Selectivity, recycle and purge reshape the methanol ranking

**a,** CO₂-to-methanol loop with a purge split. Methane formed as a side product accumulates in the loop and leaves through the purge, so CH₄ selectivity changes the loop composition as well as H₂ consumption, recycle and compression. **b,** Re/TiO₂ at 1 and 5 wt% Re (schematic; Re atoms shown in a 1:5 ratio on the same area). The four catalyst states were measured at 200 and 250 °C, 100 bar and CO₂:H₂ = 1:4.[19] **c,** The four states from measured catalyst properties (space-time yield per gram of Re, CO₂ conversion and CH₄ selectivity) through the process model at 2% purge to net production cost: **943, 962, 967 and 1,258** euros per tonne, shown in order of increasing cost. **d,** Rank probability across 5,000 draws of each state's own measurement uncertainty (CO₂ conversion and selectivities; full plant model re-solved in every draw): the economic winner keeps first place in **4,559/5,000** draws, 5 wt% Re at 250 °C stays last in **5,000/5,000**, and the second and third places exchange in **1,054/5,000**. **e,** Space-time-yield rank against net-cost rank: **ρ = 0.40**, **τ = 0.33**, with two of six pairwise comparisons inverted; the state ranked third by space-time yield per gram of Re is first economically. **f,** Net production cost over **396** purge levels from 0.5 to 40% and the Spearman ρ at each level; ρ never exceeds **0.80**. **g,** Local economic leverage at 5 wt% Re and 250 °C: CH₄ suppression **0.37579**, single-pass conversion **0.05883**, space-time yield **0.00289**.

## Figure 4 | Metal price and process optimization jointly determine the Fe-Ru ranking

**a,** NH₃ synthesis loop with the cost components associated with each unit and the decision variables *T*, *P* and *T*_sep. **b,** Catalyst beds at each optimum, drawn to scale (converter L/D = 3; pellet size illustrative): Fe **30.4 m³** at 425 °C, 105 bar and *T*_sep = 30 °C; Ru **0.066 m³** at 450 °C, 220 bar and 30 °C; Ru priced as Fe **18.2 m³** at 400 °C, 95 bar and 30 °C. The Ru bed is 463 times smaller than the Fe bed but carries 15 times its metal-inventory cost. **c,** Lowest feasible cost against synthesis pressure with *T* and *T*_sep reoptimized: Fe 11.52 at 105 bar, Ru 16.63 at 220 bar and Os 19.75 at 340 bar; dashed line, Ru priced as Fe, 10.24 at 95 bar. **d,** Ru price sweep with descriptor kinetics: Ru reoptimized over all **14,510** process states at each metal price (top), with Fe at **11.52** US dollars per tonne (dashed). Ru costs **16.63** at the model price and **10.24** priced as Fe (8 US dollars per kilogram); the two catalysts cost the same at **404 US dollars per kilogram**. Bottom, the Ru activity multiple needed for parity at each price, with full process reoptimization (**115×** at the model price); shaded, prices at which a scaling-consistent Ru descriptor reaches Fe. Supported Ru/C is compared with fused iron on measured rate laws in Fig. 5. **e,** Contribution of each cost component to the baseline **5.117** US dollars per tonne Ru − Fe difference, and the equal-price calculation: Ru at 8 US dollars per kilogram, fully reoptimized, costs **10.237**, or **1.279** below Fe. **f,** The 1,000 descriptor-uncertainty samples in (Fe *E*_N, Ru *E*_N), coloured by economic winner (Fe 653, Ru 347); dashed lines bound the Fe *E*_N interval that satisfies the 90 m³ bed limit. **g,** Outcomes across these samples: Fe feasible **79.2%**, Fe economic Top-1 **65.3%**, atomistic Top-1 equal to economic Top-1 **27.1%**, and Fe in the Top-3 **92.3%**. **h,** Ru − Fe cost difference across the **5,000** joint economic-uncertainty samples: Fe is less costly in **5,000/5,000**, with a minimum difference of **1.99** and a median of **5.46** US dollars per tonne.

## Figure 5 | With measured rate laws, supported Ru/C matches fused iron and the optimizer places it downstream

Fused iron: Model 3 of Keestra et al. with the activity of the Topsoe KM1R catalyst, fitted on commercial-iron data at 1–324 bar; promoted Ru/C (8 wt% Ru, 490 kg m⁻³): Rossetti et al., fitted at 50–100 bar. Each catalyst is optimized only inside its fitted pressure range; 95.55% Ru recovery; every metal purchase and recovered-metal credit is dated over the 25-year plant life and discounted at the Ru financing rate. **a,** Ru/C − Fe catalyst-dependent cost against the Ru financing rate in a KAAP-type loop (90 bar, −20 °C separator; solid) and with each catalyst at its own optimum (fused iron 450 °C and 110 bar, Ru/C 100 bar; dashed). Ru/C is cheaper below **4.57** and **6.49%** per year; at 0% per year the differences are **−1.21** and **−3.83**, at 12% per year **+1.95** and **+2.13** US dollars per tonne. Shaded, the 32 fixed-rate catalyst-metal leases disclosed by one refiner, 2013–2022 (**1.35–5.10%** per year); red, the reference rate, their median (**2.10%** per year), at which Ru/C is **0.65** and **1.99** US dollars per tonne cheaper. **b,** Three-bed adiabatic converter with interbed cooling at 9 MPa (−20 °C separator, 8% inerts), each bed filled with fused iron (up to 530 °C) or Ru/C (up to 460 °C): cost of the best design of each arrangement. Fused iron in the first bed with Ru/C downstream is the least-cost design from **0 to 5.46%** per year (green), an all-iron converter above; a converter with Ru/C upstream of fused iron is optimal at no rate. **c,** The least-cost converter at 2.10% per year: fused iron followed by two Ru/C beds, with bed volumes, inlet–outlet temperatures and outlet NH₃; Ru/C **35%** of the catalyst volume; **17.95** against **18.32** US dollars per tonne for the best all-iron converter. **d,** Ratio of the Ru/C rate to the fused-iron rate per kilogram of catalyst and per cubic metre of bed at 90 bar and 400 °C against the NH₃ mole fraction: per kilogram **9** at 2% and **62** at 14% NH₃, because ammonia inhibits fused iron more strongly (apparent NH₃ orders −0.89 and −0.39).

## Figure 6 | Backward design separates economic targets from physically reachable catalyst-property regions

**a,** Activity-only backward design. Ru intrinsic activity is multiplied by \(\alpha\), and the full ammonia process is reoptimized at every value. The baseline Ru activity multiplier required to reach the Fe optimum is **114.67×**. The shaded red band shows the 5th–95th percentile range of the corresponding parity target over 5,000 joint economic-uncertainty samples (**43.52×–262.28×**); the green band marks the much smaller strict-scaling activity headroom. Bottom, measured activity gains of modified Ru over a reference Ru catalyst on the same axis: Ba promotion of Ru on carbon (**75×**, turnover frequency per surface Ru), Cs promotion of Ru on carbon (**65×**), Ba–Ru/BN at 5 MPa (**>100×**) and Cs–Ru/MgO at 350 °C (**>134×**) lie within the parity band, and the Cs–Ru/MgO gain exceeds the baseline multiplier, whereas the Cs–Ru/MgO lower bound at 300 °C (**>30×**) does not decide it; Cs promotion of Ru/YSZ (**10×**, per gram of Ru), electride and oxyhydride supports give **~10×** (Ru/C12A7:e⁻ 9.8×, Ru/BaTiO₂.₅H₀.₅ 8.4× at 5 MPa), Ru/Ba–Ca(NH₂)₂ **≥33.5×** (\*against Cs–Ru/MgO),[67] and Ru confined inside carbon nanotubes **0.5×**. Circles, turnover frequency per surface site; squares, rate per mass at matched Ru content; arrows, lower bounds. **b,** Activity-only physical reachability along the strict \(E_N\) scaling relation. The minimum Ru cost is **16.455 US dollars per tonne of NH₃** at \(E_N=-1.180\) eV, remaining **4.939 US dollars per tonne** above Fe; the 673 K activity headroom is **1.023×**, and the largest state-specific gain over the process library is **1.575×**. **c,** Joint backward target region when direct Ru activity, catalyst lifetime and Ru recovery are allowed to improve together. Curves give the Ru recovery required for Fe parity for 10-, 15- and 20-year catalyst lifetimes. At 99% recovery, the conservative upper bound on the required direct activity multiplier falls to **1.291×**, **0.904×** and **0.710×**, respectively: 15- and 20-year lifetimes need no activity gain. Physical reachability is tested in d. **d,** Exact strict-scaling × lifecycle reachability over all **14,510** process states. The tested property box (catalyst lifetime ≤20 years and Ru recovery ≤99%) reaches the parity region: its best corner, 20 years and 99% recovery, gives **11.1990 US dollars per tonne of NH₃**, **0.3167 US dollars per tonne (2.75%)** below Fe. Parity requires **98.3498% recovery at 20 years** or **12.12 years of catalyst lifetime at 99% recovery**. The strict-scaling parity state occurs at approximately \(E_N=-1.190\) eV, 425 °C, 110 bar and a 30 °C separator.

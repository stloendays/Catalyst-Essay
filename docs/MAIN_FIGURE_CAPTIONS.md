## Figure 1 | Process and economic evaluation reverse the leading ammonia ranking

**a,** Atomic, laboratory and plant-level optima along the forward propagation. The atomic optimum is set by the descriptor and intrinsic turnover (N* at an fcc(211) step, *E*_N; turnover frequency at 673 K), the laboratory optimum by measured activity or space-time yield at the test conditions, and the plant-level optimum is the plant-cost leader after catalyst inventory (bed volume ≤ 90 m³), the synthesis loop (*T*, *P* and *T*_sep optimized for each catalyst) and catalyst-dependent cost. Bars mark the segment of the chain that each case measures: ammonia from the atomic to the plant-level optimum (Figs 1, 4 and 5); CO₂-to-methanol from published laboratory conversion, selectivity and space-time yield through recycle, purge and cost to the plant-level optimum (Figs 2b,d,e and 3); and the Au/TiO₂ CO-oxidation control from measured, size-dependent activity to the required catalyst mass, where the ranking is preserved (Fig. 6). The ammonia literature comparisons of Fig. 2c,f use the same laboratory-to-plant segment as methanol. The reverse arrow denotes the backward-design calculation from economic parity to the required catalyst improvement. **b,** Ru, Os and Fe step sites with N* (illustrative fcc(211) models built from each metal's bulk nearest-neighbour distance), together with the descriptor, metal price and atomic and economic ranks. **c,** Model activity volcano at 673 K for the 15 metals; the shaded region marks Ru, Os and Fe. **d,** Minimum catalyst bed volume against *E*_N; only Ru, Os and Fe satisfy the 90 m³ limit. **e,** Metal price against activity: Ru is **607 times** more active than Fe and **6,732 times** more expensive. **f,** Atomic activity rank against economic rank for all 15 metals: the leading three reverse (**Spearman ρ = −0.50**) while the full screen retains **ρ = 0.911**. **g,** Rolling Top-*K* Spearman ρ for *K* = 3–15. **h,** Catalyst-dependent cost of the three feasible metals: Fe **11.516**, Ru **16.633** and Os **19.747** US dollars per tonne of NH₃.

## Figure 2 | Published laboratory leaders are often not the plant-cost leaders

**a,** The Adaptive Catalyst Screening Agent (ACSA) reads publications and their Supporting Information, extracts every catalyst entry with the unit and location of each value (language model), reproduces the frozen hand-built cases before scoring new candidates, and uses a lower bound on cost to decide which candidates need the full optimization (from the descriptor for ammonia, from the plant cost at each purge level for methanol). Every candidate then passes through the same multiscale chain (bottom): kinetics at the active site, catalyst mass and bed volume, process optimization of the synthesis loop and the catalyst-dependent cost, from which the laboratory and plant-cost leaderboards are compared. The scientific models are unchanged. Structures are illustrations; extrudate photograph adapted from Wikimedia Commons ("Extrudates-pellets-beads-molecular-sieve-hengye-inc.jpg", CC BY-SA 4.0) and converter drawing adapted from Wikimedia Commons ("Ammoniakreaktor MS-ru.svg", CC0). **b,** Methanol: share of the within-paper comparisons (same pressure, H₂/CO₂ ratio and space velocity; **81** comparisons, **50** papers from two extractions, Supplementary Tables 1 and 1b; printed values) in which the paper's leader is not the plant-cost leader, in the inert-limited loop (CO, methane and N₂ at most 6.86% of the reactor inlet, purge optimized within that limit) and without it (open bar). First bar (STY, cat.): catalyst against catalyst, each at its best measured operating point (catalyst identity fixed before counting; Methods), the space-time-yield leader is not the plant-cost leader in **26** of the **63** comparisons with at least two catalysts (error bar: paper-cluster bootstrap 95% confidence interval, 29–53%; hatched, 19 with a cost gap above 5%). The other bars count every operating point of every catalyst, by the metric that defines the paper leaderboard: space-time yield **50/81** (49–72%), single-pass yield **50/81**, conversion **54/81**, methanol selectivity **30/81** (hatched, 42, 42, 49 and 19 above 5%); without the inlet limit, space-time yield **25/81** (17–45%; 10 above 5%). Recycled CO converted no further than reverse-water-gas-shift and CO-hydrogenation equilibrium, single-pass conversion capped at the loop's CO₂-hydrogenation equilibrium. **c,** Ammonia: the **44** within-paper comparisons (same temperature, pressure, H₂/N₂ ratio and space velocity; **31** papers; printed rate, temperature and outlet) of the 52 primary ammonia-synthesis papers. Left: the catalyst with the highest rate per gram of catalyst is not the plant-cost leader in **12** (hatched, 8 with a cost gap above 5%; error bars: paper-cluster bootstrap 95% confidence intervals, 14–42%), the catalyst with the highest rate per gram of metal in **15**, and with 90% Ru recovery in **10**. Right: what the 12 disagreements differ by, with the number of papers and the median regret: a catalyst of another metal is the plant-cost leader in **3** (2 papers; 29%), an Fe catalyst on the same support in all 3; a commercial fused-iron reference in **5** (4 papers; 22%); the same metal and content on another support or with another promoter in **2** (2 papers; 4%); the same metal at another content in **2** (2 papers; 2%). **d,e,** Two methanol comparisons: space-time yield against net production cost in the inert-limited loop (filled) and without the limit (open, joined to the same entry), with the space-time-yield leader (red) and the plant-cost leader (green) of the inert-limited loop and the purge each needs. **d,** Re/TiO₂ at 100 bar (marker area scales with CH₄ selectivity; space-time yield per gram of catalyst, whereas the paper ranks per gram of Re, by which 1 wt% Re at 250 °C leads at 6.6% regret): the leader, 5 wt% Re at 200 °C, costs **997** euros per tonne at 4.2% purge, against **921** for 1 wt% Re at 200 °C at 1.1% purge (regret 8.2%). **e,** InNi₃C₀.₅ on m-, t- and a-ZrO₂ and on SiO₂ at 300 °C and 4 MPa (Supplementary Table 1; marker area scales with CO selectivity): the space-time-yield leader on m-ZrO₂ costs **1,289** euros per tonne at 4.0% purge, against **1,138** on SiO₂ at 0.6% purge; without the limit the order reverses, **919** against **1,101**. **f,** An ammonia comparison: rate per gram of catalyst against plant cost for Ru, Fe and Co on BaTiO₃₋ₓHₓ at 5 MPa;[53] the rate leader Ru/BaTiO₂.₅H₀.₅ (2.5 wt% Ru, red) and the plant-cost leader, Fe (1 wt%, green; regret 14%).

## Figure 3 | Selectivity, recycle and purge reshape the methanol ranking

**a,** CO₂-to-methanol loop with a purge split. Methane formed as a side product accumulates in the loop and leaves through the purge, so CH₄ selectivity changes the loop composition as well as H₂ consumption, recycle and compression. **b,** Re/TiO₂ at 1 and 5 wt% Re (schematic; Re atoms shown in a 1:5 ratio on the same area). The four catalyst states were measured at 200 and 250 °C, 100 bar and CO₂:H₂ = 1:4.[19] **c,** The four states from measured catalyst properties (space-time yield per gram of Re, CO₂ conversion and CH₄ selectivity) through the process model at 2% purge to net production cost: **943, 962, 967 and 1,258** euros per tonne, shown in order of increasing cost. **d,** Rank probability across 5,000 draws of each state's own measurement uncertainty (CO₂ conversion and selectivities; full plant model re-solved in every draw): the economic winner keeps first place in **4,559/5,000** draws, 5 wt% Re at 250 °C stays last in **5,000/5,000**, and the second and third places exchange in **1,054/5,000**. **e,** Space-time-yield rank against net-cost rank: **ρ = 0.40**, **τ = 0.33**, with two of six pairwise comparisons inverted; the state ranked third by space-time yield per gram of Re is first economically. **f,** Net production cost over **396** purge levels from 0.5 to 40% and the Spearman ρ at each level; ρ never exceeds **0.80**. **g,** Local economic leverage at 5 wt% Re and 250 °C: CH₄ suppression **0.37579**, single-pass conversion **0.05883**, space-time yield **0.00289**.

## Figure 4 | Metal price and process optimization jointly determine the Fe-Ru ranking

**a,** NH₃ synthesis loop with the cost components associated with each unit and the decision variables *T*, *P* and *T*_sep. **b,** Catalyst beds at each optimum, drawn to scale (converter L/D = 3; pellet size illustrative): Fe **30.4 m³** at 425 °C, 105 bar and *T*_sep = 30 °C; Ru **0.066 m³** at 450 °C, 220 bar and 30 °C; Ru priced as Fe **18.2 m³** at 400 °C, 95 bar and 30 °C. The Ru bed is 463 times smaller than the Fe bed but carries 15 times its metal-inventory cost. **c,** Lowest feasible cost against synthesis pressure with *T* and *T*_sep reoptimized: Fe 11.52 at 105 bar, Ru 16.63 at 220 bar and Os 19.75 at 340 bar; dashed line, Ru priced as Fe, 10.24 at 95 bar. **d,** Ru at its actual catalyst cost. Top, Ru reoptimized over all **14,510** process states against the effective metal price *p*(1 − *r*)/*u*, where *u* is the gain in exposed metal of a supported catalyst over fused iron and *r* the recovered fraction. Pure Ru in the common fused-iron formulation costs **16.63** US dollars per tonne; a promoted Ru/C catalyst (*u* ≥ 11: 11% Ru dispersion against < 1% exposed Fe) costs **13.58**; with 90–95.55% recovery, the range measured for spent promoted Ru/C, it costs **11.69–11.15** at 110 bar, from 0.17 above to 0.37 below Fe. Ru matches Fe at an effective price of **404 US dollars per kilogram**. Diamonds, the same reading in the KAAP loop (90 bar, *T*_sep −20 °C): **16.39–16.27** against Fe in that loop (**16.98**, solid line). Squares, the commercial Ru/C catalyst's own bed (markers at 8 wt% Ru and 490 kg m⁻³; bars over 5–10 wt% Ru and 430–550 kg m⁻³) with 90–95.55% recovery: **12.02–11.52** (bars 11.38–12.20) in the main loop and **16.43–16.36** (bars 16.33–16.46) in the KAAP loop. Bottom, the Ru activity multiple needed for parity at each effective price, with full process reoptimization: **115×** for pure Ru, **11×** for Ru/C and **0.6–1.2×** with recovery, and **1.0–1.6×** for the own bed. Shaded, effective prices at which a scaling-consistent Ru descriptor reaches Fe (≤ **444** US dollars per kilogram). These readings charge the Ru inventory only for the metal lost at each replacement; financed over the 25-year plant life at the 12% equipment rate, Ru on its own bed costs **16.99** against **17.00** US dollars per tonne for Fe in the KAAP loop (8 wt% Ru, 490 kg m⁻³, 95.55% recovery), and it stays below Fe at any metal financing rate below 12.3% per year. **e,** Contribution of each cost component to the baseline **5.117** US dollars per tonne Ru − Fe difference, and the equal-price calculation: Ru at 8 US dollars per kilogram, fully reoptimized, costs **10.237**, or **1.279** below Fe. **f,** The 1,000 descriptor-uncertainty samples in (Fe *E*_N, Ru *E*_N), coloured by economic winner (Fe 653, Ru 347); dashed lines bound the Fe *E*_N interval that satisfies the 90 m³ bed limit. **g,** Outcomes across these samples: Fe feasible **79.2%**, Fe economic Top-1 **65.3%**, atomistic Top-1 equal to economic Top-1 **27.1%**, and Fe in the Top-3 **92.3%**. **h,** Ru − Fe cost difference across the **5,000** joint economic-uncertainty samples: Fe is less costly in **5,000/5,000**, with a minimum difference of **1.99** and a median of **5.46** US dollars per tonne.

## Figure 5 | Backward design separates economic targets from physically reachable catalyst-property regions

**a,** Activity-only backward design. Ru intrinsic activity is multiplied by \(\alpha\), and the full ammonia process is reoptimized at every value. The baseline Ru activity multiplier required to reach the Fe optimum is **114.67×**. The shaded red band shows the 5th–95th percentile range of the corresponding parity target over 5,000 joint economic-uncertainty samples (**43.52×–262.28×**); the green band marks the much smaller strict-scaling activity headroom. Bottom, measured activity gains of modified Ru over a reference Ru catalyst on the same axis: Ba promotion of Ru on carbon (**75×**, turnover frequency per surface Ru), Cs promotion of Ru on carbon (**65×**), Ba–Ru/BN at 5 MPa (**>100×**) and Cs–Ru/MgO at 350 °C (**>134×**) lie within the parity band, and the Cs–Ru/MgO gain exceeds the baseline multiplier, whereas the Cs–Ru/MgO lower bound at 300 °C (**>30×**) does not decide it; Cs promotion of Ru/YSZ (**10×**, per gram of Ru), electride and oxyhydride supports give **~10×** (Ru/C12A7:e⁻ 9.8×, Ru/BaTiO₂.₅H₀.₅ 8.4× at 5 MPa), Ru/Ba–Ca(NH₂)₂ **≥33.5×** (\*against Cs–Ru/MgO),[67] and Ru confined inside carbon nanotubes **0.5×**. Circles, turnover frequency per surface site; squares, rate per mass at matched Ru content; arrows, lower bounds. **b,** Activity-only physical reachability along the strict \(E_N\) scaling relation. The minimum Ru cost is **16.455 US dollars per tonne of NH₃** at \(E_N=-1.180\) eV, remaining **4.939 US dollars per tonne** above Fe; the 673 K activity headroom is **1.023×**, and the largest state-specific gain over the process library is **1.575×**. **c,** Joint backward target region when direct Ru activity, catalyst lifetime and Ru recovery are allowed to improve together. Curves give the Ru recovery required for Fe parity for 10-, 15- and 20-year catalyst lifetimes. At 99% recovery, the conservative upper bound on the required direct activity multiplier falls to **1.291×**, **0.904×** and **0.710×**, respectively: 15- and 20-year lifetimes need no activity gain. Physical reachability is tested in d. **d,** Exact strict-scaling × lifecycle reachability over all **14,510** process states. The tested property box (catalyst lifetime ≤20 years and Ru recovery ≤99%) reaches the parity region: its best corner, 20 years and 99% recovery, gives **11.1990 US dollars per tonne of NH₃**, **0.3167 US dollars per tonne (2.75%)** below Fe. Parity requires **98.3498% recovery at 20 years** or **12.12 years of catalyst lifetime at 99% recovery**. The strict-scaling parity state occurs at approximately \(E_N=-1.190\) eV, 425 °C, 110 bar and a 30 °C separator.


## Figure 6 | Catalyst effects on process operation determine whether rankings change

**a,** Routes by which catalyst properties affect each reaction-specific economic objective. NH₃: metal price and intrinsic activity determine catalyst inventory and preferred operating conditions, which alter compression, reactor and equipment costs. Methanol: CH₄ selectivity, conversion and space-time yield affect loop composition, recycle, purge losses and catalyst requirement. Absolute costs are not compared across reactions. **b,** Au/TiO₂ CO-oxidation control: 2–6 nm Au particles on the same support, drawn to scale (illustrative particle shapes). **c,** Activity rank against catalyst-mass rank: no crossings, **ρ = τ = 1.000**, with the complete order preserved in **10,000/10,000** parameter samples drawn from ranges reported in the literature; 6 nm particles require **8.064 times** as much catalyst as 2 nm particles. **d,** Mass activity and required catalyst mass against Au diameter. **e,** Additional analysis allowing mild, moderate and strong candidate-specific kinetic and operating variation over 273–293 K and 273–313 K (moderate variation, 273–293 K: **92.16%** exact rank preservation). **f,** Within-system Spearman rank agreement after each system's downstream mapping: NH₃, ρ = 0.911 across all 15 metals and −0.50 for the three atomic-activity leaders; the four-state methanol purge example, ρ = 0.40 at 2% purge (−0.6 to 0.8 over 0.5–40% purge); and the fixed-condition Au/TiO₂ control, ρ = 1.000 (mean ρ = 0.992 under moderate candidate-specific variation).

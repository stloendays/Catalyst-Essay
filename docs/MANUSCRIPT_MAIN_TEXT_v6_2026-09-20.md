# Multiscale economics reshapes catalyst rankings at the industrial decision frontier

## Abstract

Catalyst discovery is commonly organized around atomistic descriptors and intrinsic rates, whereas industrial selection is made after those properties propagate through kinetics, catalyst inventory, reactor operation, recycle, separation and cost. Here we treat catalyst screening as a bidirectional decision problem: forward propagation tests whether an upstream ranking survives industrial optimization, while backward design asks what catalyst improvement is required to overturn the downstream decision and whether that target is physically reachable. In ammonia synthesis, the atomic order Ru > Os > Fe becomes Fe > Ru > Os after candidate-specific process and economic optimization. Equalizing the Ru and Fe metal prices and reoptimizing the complete process reverses that order, with Ru reaching 14.712 versus 15.292 US dollars per tonne of NH3 for Fe, showing that the inversion arises from metal cost coupled to process reoptimization. Around the canonical price regime, however, Fe remains lower-cost in all 5,000 preregistered joint cost-uncertainty draws. Backward design places the canonical Ru activity-parity requirement at 201-fold, far beyond the 2.525-fold maximum scaling-consistent headroom. In CO2-to-methanol, Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states are reshaped through a selectivity-recycle pathway, whereas a literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 control preserves the upstream order. A frozen decision-aware agent benchmark further shows that complete industrial decisions can be recovered below a 206-CU deterministic-policy threshold, with a 22-CU protocol-complete oracle defining the irreducible reference. Together, these results link catalyst ranking, process coupling, physical reachability and scientific-compute allocation at the industrial decision frontier.

## Introduction

Computational catalyst discovery increasingly relies on adsorption energies, reactivity descriptors, scaling relations, volcano relations and surrogate models to rank large materials spaces.[1–6] Descriptor-based microkinetic frameworks have made these relations operational across broad catalyst families, while first-principles multiscale modelling has connected elementary-step energetics to experimentally relevant rates and conditions.[6,7] Earlier work linked first-principles ammonia descriptors directly to reactor conditions and catalyst selection,[32] and optimization frameworks formalized mechanistic modelling and catalyst design as coupled optimization problems.[33,36] Catalyst-testing studies have also shown that relative rankings can depend on the reactor or test environment; in fluid catalytic cracking, laboratory reactor choice can produce erroneous catalyst rankings relative to more representative operation.[35] The unresolved question addressed here is therefore narrower: when an atomistic screen is propagated through candidate-specific process optimization and economics, does the *ordering among discrete catalyst candidates* remain faithful to the industrial decision?

This question is most consequential near the decision frontier. Screening is comparative, and a model may remain well correlated across a broad candidate population while changing the identity of the leading candidate. Global predictive quality therefore does not guarantee fidelity where experimental and computational resources are concentrated. The same issue arises under uncertainty: adaptive-sampling and Bayesian-error approaches have shown that uncertainty should be interpreted relative to the quantity or decision being resolved, rather than treated as a uniform reason to refine every part of a model.[8–10]

We formulate this problem in two directions. Forward propagation maps catalyst properties through kinetics and process optimization to an industrial objective. Backward design starts from an economic target, identifies the catalyst-property improvement required to reach it, and tests whether that target lies within an admissible catalyst-property manifold. Integrated material-and-process design has previously shown how process objectives can be inverted into idealized molecular targets,[34] while catalyst-design frameworks have optimized descriptors directly inside kinetic models.[33,36] Our focus is candidate-level parity and reachability: which catalyst wins after industrial propagation, what property shift would be required to overturn that decision, and whether that shift is compatible with the catalyst manifold represented by the underlying scaling relations.

We apply the framework to two reaction systems and one rank-preservation control. Ammonia synthesis probes an activity-inventory/reactor-demand pathway across a 15-metal screen grounded in established scaling, Brønsted-Evans-Polanyi and volcano relations for transition-metal ammonia synthesis.[5,11,12] CO2-to-methanol probes a selectivity-recycle pathway using literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states together with an explicit process loop; this reaction class is strongly conditioned by conversion, selectivity, recycle and separation choices at process scale.[13–15] Au/TiO2 CO oxidation provides a counterfactual with a literature-calibrated monotonic size-activity mapping and a long-established sensitivity of low-temperature CO oxidation to supported Au particle size.[16–19]

We then embed the same decision chain in a frozen scientific-action environment. Tool-using language-model agents, autonomous chemical platforms and Bayesian experiment-selection methods have shown that scientific workflows can be organized around iterative action selection rather than one-shot prediction.[20–27] Recent catalyst-focused systems extend this idea toward literature-guided optimization, reaction-network construction, tool orchestration and verification-aware scientific workflows.[28–31] Resource-aware scientific-agent evaluation now also measures the cost of simulation actions rather than only answer correctness,[37] and a recent preprint frames active learning directly by downstream decision value for process scale-up.[38] DISCOVER addresses a complementary question: whether a language-model policy can allocate a finite scientific-compute budget across a fixed deterministic decision chain so that the industrial decision converges before every intermediate quantitative target does.

Across the three catalytic systems, multiscale propagation spans a continuum from exact rank preservation to local reshuffling and decision-frontier inversion. The governing variable is the strength and topology of catalyst-specific coupling to downstream process decisions. We therefore separate three questions that are often conflated in screening studies: whether a ranking changes, which catalyst-to-process pathway causes the change, and whether an economically useful catalyst target lies inside the admissible material manifold. The agent benchmark then applies the same decision logic to computation: additional compute is valuable only when it resolves a part of the chain that can still change the industrial decision.

## Results

### Industrial propagation reverses the ammonia decision frontier

The 15-metal ammonia screen places Ru, Os and Fe first, second and third in atomic activity, consistent with the established activity landscape for transition-metal ammonia synthesis.[11,12] After candidate-specific process optimization and evaluation of the catalyst-dependent economic objective, the order becomes Fe, Ru and Os, with costs of 15.292, 22.031 and 25.832 US dollars per tonne of NH3, respectively (Fig. 1).

The reversal is concentrated at the decision frontier rather than distributed across the full screen. For the three leading atomic candidates, Spearman rho is -0.50 and Kendall tau is -0.33; across all 15 metals, the raw Spearman correlation is 0.929 (Fig. 2). Thus the atomistic screen retains strong global structure while selecting a different leading candidate among the materials most likely to be pursued. Frontier-local rank fidelity, rather than full-set correlation alone, determines whether the screen supports the intended industrial choice.

### Process reoptimization and uncertainty resolve the ammonia mechanism

Candidate-specific process freedom does not restore the atomic order. With pressure-dependent equipment capital included, Fe occupies an interior optimum near 425 °C and 180 bar with a 30 °C separator, whereas Ru prefers approximately 450 °C and 425 bar with a 25 °C separator; Os retains a broad shallow high-pressure minimum (Fig. 4). The candidates therefore enter different operating regimes before the economic ranking is evaluated.

Descriptor uncertainty acts on several downstream endpoints rather than on a single ranking statistic. Across 1,000 frozen descriptor-uncertainty draws, Fe is feasible under the 90 m3 bed-volume criterion in 79.9% of realizations, is the economic Top-1 candidate in 68.1% (681/1000), and remains within the actionable Top-3 in 94.0%. The previously reported 28.2% Top-1 survival is a different quantity: it is the probability that the atomic Top-1 and economic Top-1 identities coincide (Fig. 3a). This separation distinguishes candidate-specific decision stability from preservation of the upstream winner.

A direct price counterfactual resolves the causal role of the Ru price premium. Holding every non-price input, constraint and costing convention fixed, we set the Ru metal price equal to the Fe value of 8 US dollars per kilogram and reoptimized Ru over the full 14,136-state process library. The Ru optimum shifts from 450 °C and 425 bar to 425 °C and 170 bar, and the optimized cost falls to 14.712 US dollars per tonne of NH3, 0.580 US dollars per tonne below Fe. The Fe-over-Ru inversion therefore does not survive metal-price equalization. Metal cost and process optimization are coupled: changing the catalyst price changes the preferred process regime rather than simply subtracting a static inventory term.

The canonical 6.739 US dollars per tonne Ru-Fe gap is distributed across several cost pools. Fresh-feed compression contributes +4.632 US dollars per tonne, metal inventory +1.763, compressor capital +1.181 and refrigeration +0.629, while vessel pressure, recycle compression and reactor-base terms offset part of the gap. This decomposition explains where the baseline difference resides; the reoptimized equal-price counterfactual identifies how the system responds when the price driver is changed.

The local economic decision remains stable within the preregistered cost-uncertainty envelope. Across 5,000 draws that jointly vary metal price, the CAPEX coefficient, electricity price and catalyst lifetime, full process reoptimization gives P(C_Fe < C_Ru) = 1.000, with the smallest sampled Ru-Fe cost gap equal to 2.382 US dollars per tonne (Fig. 3b). The corresponding Ru activity-parity distribution spans 70.78-fold, 174.27-fold and 462.00-fold at the 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles, respectively (Fig. 3c). The equal-price reversal and the joint Monte Carlo therefore answer different questions: the former identifies a causal boundary, whereas the latter shows robustness around the canonical economic regime.

### Backward design separates economic requirement from physical reachability

Forward propagation identifies the preferred catalyst but does not reveal how far a losing candidate lies from parity. We therefore increased Ru intrinsic activity and reoptimized the full process at each multiplier. Under the canonical economic parameters, Ru reaches the Fe cost reference only after a 201.22-fold activity increase (Fig. 5).

The corresponding scaling-consistent design space is much narrower. Accessible activity headroom is 1.090-fold at 673 K and reaches a maximum of 2.525-fold over the frozen process-state library. Under strict scaling, the minimum Ru cost remains 21.398 US dollars per tonne of NH3 at E_N = -1.215 eV, above Fe (Fig. 6). The activity-only route to parity therefore lies far outside the current scaling-consistent manifold. Even after economic uncertainty broadens the parity target, the 5th-percentile requirement of 70.78-fold remains more than an order of magnitude above the 2.525-fold maximum headroom.

Backward design therefore converts an industrial disadvantage into a quantitative catalyst requirement and immediately tests whether that requirement is compatible with the material class represented by the scaling relation. For the present ammonia system, incremental intrinsic-activity improvement within the frozen scaling manifold cannot close the modeled economic gap.

### Methanol ranking reshaping follows a selectivity-recycle pathway

The framework was transferred to a distinct process architecture using four literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states.[13] With space-time yield per gram of Re as the upstream metric, the order is 1 wt% Re at 250 °C, 1 wt% Re at 200 °C, 5 wt% Re at 200 °C and 5 wt% Re at 250 °C. After propagation through the explicit recycle and separation model at the source-anchored 2% purge condition, the economic order becomes 5 wt% Re at 200 °C, 1 wt% Re at 200 °C, 1 wt% Re at 250 °C and 5 wt% Re at 250 °C, with net production costs of 943, 967, 975 and 1,258 euros per tonne, respectively (Fig. 7).

The Re-normalized upstream winner falls from first to third. Across the four states, Spearman rho is 0.20, Kendall tau is 0.00 and three of six pairwise comparisons invert. The mechanism differs from ammonia. Methane formation consumes reactants and increases gas accumulation, purge, recycle and compression burden. At the 5 wt% Re, 250 °C benchmark, local economic leverage is 0.37579 for methane suppression, 0.05883 for single-pass conversion and 0.00289 for space-time yield (Fig. 8). The process sensitivity is consistent with the broader CO2-to-methanol literature, in which equilibrium-limited conversion, recycle architecture and separation burden materially affect plant-level performance.[14,15]

The reshuffling persists across the available loop degree of freedom. Sweeping purge from 0.5% to 40% over 396 levels leaves the Re-normalized upstream winner economically suboptimal at every purge fraction; rho never exceeds 0.40 and at least two of six pairs remain inverted. Candidate-specific purge optimization changes the detailed order but does not restore the upstream ranking.

The four-candidate economic order also remains unchanged in all 5,000 draws of the cost-parameter analysis. Because the canonical D01 objective excludes Re purchase and replacement, metal-price and lifetime perturbations are structurally inactive in the canonical boundary calculation. A separately labelled active-Re replacement extension, using STY-derived Re inventory, likewise preserves the same four-state order in all 5,000 draws (Fig. 3d). The selectivity-recycle ranking is therefore stable to the tested economic perturbations without redefining the canonical D01 objective.

### Coupling topology explains cross-reaction transfer

Ammonia and methanol expose two distinct routes from catalyst properties to process cost (Fig. 9a). In ammonia, activity and catalyst price act through catalyst inventory, reactor demand and the preferred operating regime. In methanol, selectivity acts through reactant loss, gas accumulation, purge, recycle and compression. The relevant upstream variable therefore depends on which downstream cost pools the catalyst property can move.

This comparison argues against a universal scalar screening heuristic across reactions. Transfer should instead begin from the catalyst-to-process coupling topology: identify the property that controls the dominant process pathway, propagate that property to the industrial objective, and only then decide which descriptor or experiment is decision-relevant.

### A rank-preservation control shows that inversion is conditional

A literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 CO-oxidation control tests the complementary regime. Low-temperature CO oxidation on supported Au has long shown pronounced particle-size and support effects,[16,17] and the present control uses an absolute-rate anchor from Janssens et al.[18] together with the closest-loading size dependence from Overbury et al.[19] Candidate states differ only in Au particle size from 2 to 6 nm under a common operating condition and process topology.

Under this common monotonic mapping, intrinsic activity and required catalyst burden preserve the same order, 2 > 3 > 4 > 5 > 6 nm. Spearman rho and Kendall tau are both 1.000, no pairwise comparison inverts, and all 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws preserve the complete order (Fig. 9b). The 6 nm state requires 8.064 times the catalyst burden of the 2 nm state.

A semi-open extension allows candidate-specific kinetic perturbations and optimization over literature-constrained temperature and feed-ratio windows. Exact ordering is preserved in 92.16% of 10,000 draws over 273.15–293.15 K, with mean rho = 0.99214. Extending the window to 313.15 K lowers exact preservation to 72.62% while retaining mean rho = 0.96802. Multiscale propagation therefore does not generate inversion by construction: ranking changes emerge when candidate-specific downstream coupling becomes strong enough to compete with the upstream separation.

### Decision-aware compute allocation has a capability-bounded operating envelope

We next asked whether computation itself could be allocated according to decision value. DISCOVER V1 exposes a frozen 11-action scientific interface over the same multiscale decision chain. The language-model policy selects admissible actions under a finite compute budget; all numerical physics and economics are executed by deterministic tools. The primary endpoint requires the correct economic winner, decision-pair ordering and reachability classification, while numerical recovery of the backward parity multiplier is scored separately.

On the anonymous closed-book benchmark, complete-decision recovery is 6/35 for the nano tier, 15/35 for the mini tier and 35/35 for the strong tier. The deterministic fixed policy completes at 206 compute units (CU). In the boundary study, the strong model reaches 20/20 complete decisions at a 75-CU allowance, with a median 52.5 CU to the ledger-true decision-stable point; at 50 CU, completion falls to 13/20 even though winner and decision-pair identification remain correct in all 20 runs (Fig. 10a,c).

A deterministic oracle places these numbers on an absolute scale. The literal scorer-complete floor is 7 CU, but it can exploit incomplete screening. The protocol-complete S1-S3 oracle requires 22 CU and includes a process window, all-candidate activity screening, optimization of the minimum unresolved Fe/Ru/Os set, backward design and reachability. Relative to this scientific lower bound, the strong 75-CU allowance is 3.41 times oracle, its median 52.5-CU decision-stable spend is 2.39 times oracle, and the fixed-policy threshold is 9.36 times oracle (Fig. 10c).

Decision convergence precedes quantitative-target convergence. The canonical Ru-to-Fe parity multiplier is recovered in 9/20 runs at 75 CU and 20/20 at 225 CU. Thus a complete industrial decision can close before every intermediate numerical quantity converges, provided that the residual quantitative error does not cross the reachability boundary.

The below-threshold mechanism is adaptive scope allocation. At constrained budgets the strong tier repeatedly constructs and uses process windows smaller than the full 14,136-state domain, whereas canonical narrow-window use is 0/20 at 225 CU and 0/9 at 250 CU. Under a non-binding 5,000-CU allowance, narrow-window allocation is again 0/20, median decision stabilization moves to 566 CU, and the policy spends a further median 148 CU before self-stop, producing 714 CU median final spend (Fig. 10b,c). Budget pressure therefore activates search compression; when that pressure disappears, the decision itself stabilizes much later before additional post-stability computation is consumed.

The adaptive advantage is consequently localized to a capability- and budget-dependent regime below the fixed-policy completion threshold. It is a decision-recovery advantage under constrained scientific compute, not a universal reduction in raw compute.

## Discussion

Catalyst screening becomes a decision problem when atomistic differences are propagated through the process variables that determine industrial cost. The present results show that the relevant failure mode is often local: a broad catalyst screen can remain strongly correlated while the leading candidates reorder at the decision frontier. Descriptor quality and industrial decision fidelity should therefore be evaluated separately, with particular attention to the candidates and feasibility boundaries that can change the downstream choice.

The ammonia counterfactual identifies how price and process interact in that decision. Equalizing the Ru and Fe metal prices reverses the economic order, so the Fe-over-Ru result cannot be attributed to process penalties independently of the Ru price premium. At the same time, the response is not a static metal-cost subtraction. Price equalization moves Ru from a 425-bar optimum to 170 bar and reorganizes compression and capital burdens. The operative mechanism is therefore a coupled one: catalyst economics changes the process optimum, and the process optimum determines how the economic consequence is expressed.

This causal boundary is distinct from local decision robustness. Within the preregistered four-parameter cost envelope around the canonical regime, Fe remains lower-cost than Ru in all 5,000 draws, while the descriptor Monte Carlo gives a 68.1% probability that Fe is the economic Top-1 candidate. These two uncertainty analyses interrogate different layers of the decision chain. Reporting uncertainty at the variable where it enters is insufficient; the more useful quantity is the probability that the propagated uncertainty changes feasibility, winner identity, rank order or reachability.

Backward design turns the downstream gap into a catalyst-design requirement. Integrated material/process optimization has previously translated process objectives into molecular targets,[34] and catalyst-design frameworks have optimized descriptors within kinetic models.[33,36] Here the target is defined by explicit candidate-level economic parity and then tested against the scaling-consistent catalyst manifold. The canonical 201.22-fold Ru activity requirement, together with the 2.525-fold maximum headroom, separates an economically useful direction from one that is reachable by incremental movement along the current descriptor path. This distinction provides a direct stopping rule for activity-only optimization and motivates changes in material class, stability, selectivity or process architecture when the backward target lies outside the accessible manifold.

Reaction transfer follows the same logic but not the same mechanism. Ammonia couples activity and catalyst price to inventory, reactor demand and operating severity; methanol couples selectivity to feed loss, purge, recycle and compression. The Au/TiO2 control closes the causal argument by showing that multiscale propagation can preserve an upstream order when candidates share a common monotonic downstream mapping. The relevant transferable object is therefore the coupling topology between catalyst properties and process cost pools, not a universal descriptor or a universal expectation of inversion.

The agent benchmark extends this decision-centered view to computation. Tool-using language models and autonomous chemical systems have established that scientific workflows can be organized around iterative action selection,[20–31] while cost-aware evaluation and decision-focused active learning make the resource and downstream-objective dimensions explicit.[37,38] DISCOVER isolates a narrower question: how much of a fixed multiscale decision chain must be executed before the industrial endpoint is stable? The 22-CU protocol-complete oracle provides the irreducible reference, placing the strong tier's 52.5-CU median decision-stable spend at the 75-CU cell within 2.39 times the shortest scientifically complete chain, compared with 9.36 times oracle for the deterministic 206-CU policy.

The upper boundary is equally informative. When the compute allowance becomes non-binding, narrow-window allocation disappears, median decision stabilization is delayed to 566 CU, and a further 148 CU is spent before self-stop. The failure mode therefore begins before post-stability overspending: without budget pressure, the policy stops compressing the process search itself. The practical Agent result is an operating envelope in which model capability, budget and the unresolved decision state jointly determine whether adaptive allocation is useful.

Forward propagation, backward reachability and decision-aware compute allocation thus form one decision-centered workflow. Forward analysis tests whether an upstream ranking survives industrial propagation; causal counterfactuals identify which economic or process variables generate the boundary; backward analysis converts the resulting gap into a catalyst-property target and tests physical reachability; adaptive allocation then prioritizes the remaining scientific actions that can still change the decision. This workflow preserves atomistic insight while evaluating it against the industrial objective for which catalyst selection is ultimately made.

## Methods

### Multiscale decision analysis

Each candidate is propagated through a frozen chain linking catalyst-level inputs to a process-level economic objective. Rankings before and after propagation are compared using Spearman and Kendall correlations, pairwise inversions and top-rank identity. Frontier analyses focus on leading upstream candidates while full-set statistics distinguish local decision changes from global rank disorder. Backward design varies a selected catalyst property, reoptimizes the downstream process and identifies the value required for economic parity, which is then compared with the reachable catalyst-property or scaling manifold.

### Ammonia synthesis

The ammonia analysis uses NH3-FINAL-1.1. The 15-metal screen is propagated through frozen scaling and kinetic relations, catalyst productivity and inventory, candidate-specific process-state optimization, pressure-dependent equipment capital and seven catalyst-dependent economic cost pools. Temperature, pressure and separator temperature are reoptimized per candidate over the full 14,136-state process library. Descriptor uncertainty is propagated through 1,000 frozen draws; Fe feasibility is evaluated against the 90 m3 bed-volume criterion, and economic-winner, Top-1-survival and Top-3-actionable outcomes are retained separately.

The Ru price counterfactual changes only the Ru metal purchase price, setting it equal to the Fe value of 8 US dollars per kilogram, while all kinetic, engineering, costing and optimization conventions remain unchanged; Ru is then fully reoptimized over the same process library. The canonical Fe-Ru cost gap is decomposed over the same seven cost pools.

The preregistered cost-side Monte Carlo uses 5,000 draws with seed 20260920. Fe and Ru metal-price multipliers are sampled independently from a log-uniform distribution over 0.5-2.0. A common CAPEX multiplier is sampled uniformly over 0.8-1.2, electricity price uniformly over 20-100 US dollars per megawatt-hour, and catalyst lifetime uniformly over 5-20 years. Fe and Ru are reoptimized for every draw. The Ru activity-parity multiplier alpha* is recomputed against the draw-specific reoptimized Fe target.

The canonical Ru backward-design calculation varies intrinsic activity with full downstream reoptimization, and reachability is evaluated over the frozen descriptor path and process-state library. The mechanistic and scaling basis follows the established transition-metal ammonia-synthesis literature.[5,11,12]

### CO2-to-methanol

The methanol analysis uses the restored D01 v3 recycle/separation model and four Re/TiO2 states derived from the 1 and 5 wt% catalysts reported by Gothe et al.[13] The canonical comparison uses 2% purge. Upstream rankings are reconstructed from space-time yield per gram of Re, single-pass methanol yield and space-time yield per gram of catalyst; downstream ranking uses the frozen near-full-plant net production cost. Re purchase price and catalyst replacement are excluded from the canonical objective. Purge robustness spans 0.5-40% over 396 levels, with additional candidate-specific purge reoptimization. Local leverage for space-time yield, conversion and methane suppression is evaluated at the 5 wt% Re, 250 °C benchmark. The explicit-loop interpretation is consistent with established CO2-to-methanol process assessments in which recycle, compression and separation contribute materially to plant performance.[14,15]

The 5,000-draw economic-parameter analysis propagates the sampled CAPEX and electricity terms through the candidate-specific D01 accounting while retaining the canonical objective definition. Because Re purchase and replacement are absent from that boundary, sampled metal-price and lifetime variables are structurally inactive in the canonical calculation. A separately labelled extension adds active-Re replacement using STY-derived Re inventory and the frozen Re price, with the same sampled price and lifetime multipliers; this extension is reported as a robustness analysis and does not replace MEOH-D01-v3.

### Au/TiO2 rank-preservation control

The canonical control compares 2–6 nm Au/TiO2 states under a common fixed condition and process topology. The reference rate, Au loading and dispersion are anchored to Janssens et al.,[18] and the nominal size exponent to the 4.5 wt% Au/TiO2 series of Overbury et al.[19] The broader physical basis for low-temperature supported-Au activity and size sensitivity follows the established Au/TiO2 literature.[16,17] Rank preservation is evaluated in 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws. A semi-open extension perturbs candidate kinetics and independently optimizes temperature and O2/CO ratio within literature-constrained windows.

### Decision-aware agent benchmark

DISCOVER V1 exposes a frozen 11-action interface. The agent receives a finite CU budget and selects admissible actions; the environment executes all numerical work. The primary endpoint is `winner_correct ∧ pair_decision_correct ∧ reachability_correct`; backward parity-multiplier accuracy is scored independently. The deterministic fixed-policy reference completes at 206 CU. Cross-model tests use the frozen anonymous closed-book task. DISCOVER-BOUNDARY-C1 probes the strong tier around the fixed-policy boundary, the weaker-tier transfer cells and a non-binding 5,000-CU allowance. Narrow-window allocation requires a process window smaller than the 14,136-state domain followed by a successful scoped action using that window. Decision-stable CU is the cumulative ledger spend from which the complete endpoint remains correct through the final action.

Two deterministic oracle lower bounds are retained. The 7-CU scorer-complete floor is the shortest legal action chain that yields a scorer-correct winner, decision pair and reachability classification when the oracle may select only decision-relevant candidates. The manuscript-facing protocol-complete oracle additionally satisfies the S1-S3 scientific stopping criteria: it constructs a valid process window, computes activity for all 15 candidates, resolves the minimum Fe/Ru/Os process set, performs backward design and closes reachability. The resulting minimum is 22 CU. All Agent headline values are reproduced from retained raw traces with the frozen scorer and ledger; clean-checkout CI verifies source hashes and replays the scorer without invoking an LLM or generating new traces.

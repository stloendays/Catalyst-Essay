## Figure 1 | A globally correlated catalyst screen inverts at the industrial decision frontier

**a,** Forward propagation from the atomic descriptor (N* at an fcc(211) step, *E*_N) through microkinetics (turnover frequency at 673 K), catalyst inventory (bed volume ≤ 90 m³) and the synthesis loop (*T*, *P* and *T*_sep reoptimized for each catalyst) to the catalyst-dependent cost, and the backward-design question that returns from cost to catalyst. **b,** Ru, Os and Fe step sites with N* (illustrative fcc(211) models built from each metal's bulk nearest-neighbour distance), with descriptor, metal price and the atomic → economic rank. **c,** Model activity volcano at 673 K with the 15 metals; the shaded band marks the decision frontier. **d,** Minimum catalyst bed volume against *E*_N; only Ru, Os and Fe fit within the 90 m³ limit. **e,** Metal price against activity: Ru is **72 times** more active than Fe and **6,732 times** more expensive. **f,** Atomic activity rank against economic rank for all 15 metals: the leading three invert (**Spearman ρ = −0.50**) while the full screen retains **ρ = 0.929**. **g,** Rolling Top-*K* Spearman ρ, *K* = 3–15. **h,** Catalyst-dependent cost of the three feasible metals: Fe **15.292**, Ru **22.031** and Os **25.832** US dollars per tonne of NH₃; the economic winner is the third-ranked atomic candidate.

## Figure 2 | Metal cost and process reoptimization jointly set the ammonia decision boundary

**a,** NH₃ synthesis loop with the cost pools each unit carries and the decision variables *T*, *P* and *T*_sep. **b,** Catalyst beds at each optimum, drawn to scale (converter L/D = 3; pellet size illustrative): Fe **17.1 m³** at 425 °C, 180 bar, *T*_sep 30 °C; Ru **0.066 m³** at 450 °C, 425 bar, 25 °C; Ru priced as Fe **9.4 m³** at 425 °C, 170 bar, 30 °C. The Ru bed is 258 times smaller than the Fe bed yet carries 26 times its metal-inventory cost. **c,** Lowest feasible cost against synthesis pressure with *T* and *T*_sep reoptimized: Fe 15.29 at 180 bar, Ru 22.03 and Os 25.83 at 425 bar; dashed, Ru priced as Fe, 14.71 at 170 bar. **d,** Ru reoptimized over all **14,136** process states as its metal price varies from 1 to 3 × 10⁵ US dollars per kilogram. Ru matches the Fe optimum at **164 US dollars per kilogram**, and its optimal pressure rises from 170 to 420–430 bar as the metal becomes dearer. **e,** The canonical **6.739** US dollars per tonne Ru − Fe gap by cost pool, and the equal-price intervention: Ru at 8 US dollars per kilogram, fully reoptimized, costs **14.712**, **0.580** below Fe. **f,** The 1,000 frozen descriptor draws in (Fe *E*_N, Ru *E*_N), coloured by economic winner (Fe 681, Ru 319); dashed lines bound the Fe *E*_N interval in which Fe meets the 90 m³ bed limit. **g,** Decision endpoints across those draws: Fe feasible **79.9%**, Fe economic Top-1 **68.1%**, atomic Top-1 equal to economic Top-1 **28.2%**, Fe in the actionable Top-3 **94.0%**. **h,** Ru − Fe cost gap across the **5,000** preregistered joint cost draws: Fe is cheaper in **5,000/5,000**, with a minimum gap of **2.38** and a median of **7.11** US dollars per tonne.

## Figure 4 | Methanol catalyst states re-rank through a selectivity–recycle pathway

**a,** CO₂-to-methanol loop with a purge split. Methane formed as a side product accumulates in the loop and leaves only with the purge, so CH₄ selectivity acts through the CH₄ content of the loop gas and through recycle and purge losses on H₂ feed and compression. **b,** Re/TiO₂ at 1 and 5 wt% Re (schematic; Re atoms in the ratio 1 : 5 on the same area). The four catalyst states were measured at 200 and 250 °C, 100 bar and CO₂ : H₂ = 1 : 4.[13] **c,** The four states from measured catalyst metrics (space-time yield per gram of Re, CO₂ conversion, CH₄ selectivity) through the loop at 2% purge (CH₄ fraction of the loop gas, recycle flow, H₂ feed cost) to net production cost: **943, 967, 975 and 1,258** euros per tonne, rows in net-cost order. **d,** Rank probability across the 5,000-draw cost analysis: every state keeps its canonical rank in **5,000/5,000** draws, for the canonical D01 boundary and for the active-Re replacement extension. **e,** Space-time-yield rank against net-cost rank: **ρ = 0.20**, **τ = 0.00**, three of six pairs inverted; the state ranked third upstream is first economically. **f,** Net production cost over **396** purge levels from 0.5 to 40% and the Spearman ρ at each; ρ never exceeds **0.40**. **g,** Local economic leverage at 5 wt% Re, 250 °C: CH₄ suppression **0.37579**, single-pass conversion **0.05883**, space-time yield **0.00289**.

## Figure 5 | Catalyst-to-process coupling topology decides whether an upstream ranking reshapes or survives

## Figure 6 | Decision-aware agents make the multiscale decision framework repeatedly executable

**a,** The agent: a language-model policy (strong, mini and nano tiers) chooses typed actions priced in compute units (CU; 1 CU is the wall time of 1,000 microkinetic state evaluations); deterministic S1–S3 tools on the frozen NH3-FINAL-1.1 model perform all physics and economics, and a ledger and scorer define the complete decision (winner, decision pair and reachability). **b,** Three chains to the same complete decision: the protocol-complete oracle (**22 CU**); one strong-tier run at the 150-CU allowance that builds 1,302- and 1,784-state process windows, holds the correct winner from 65 CU and completes at **102 CU**; and fixed policy D, which spends 111 CU on the full 14,136-state window and completes at **206 CU**. **c,** Complete-decision recovery against compute allowance for the three tiers, with a Wilson 95% band for the strong tier (*n* = 20 per cell, except strong 200 CU, *n* = 8, and 250 CU, *n* = 9); under the non-binding 5,000-CU allowance the strong tier completes 20/20. **d,** Strong-tier runs using a narrow process window and the median smallest window built; under the non-binding allowance, 0/20. **e,** Median decision-stable and final spend against allowance, with the 22-CU oracle and D's 206 CU; non-binding, **566** and **714** CU. **f,** Spend in units of the protocol oracle: 75-CU cell decision-stable **2.39×**, 75-CU allowance **3.41×**, fixed policy D **9.36×**, non-binding decision-stable **25.73×**. **g,** Action errors and empty turns by layer at 175 CU (20 runs per cell), including the mini tier's typed-interface arm.

# Multiscale economics reshapes catalyst rankings at the industrial decision frontier

## Abstract

Catalyst discovery commonly ranks materials by atomistic descriptors and intrinsic rates, whereas industrial selection is made after those properties propagate through kinetics, catalyst inventory, process operation and cost. Here we treat catalyst screening as a decision problem and ask when an upstream ranking survives industrial propagation, which catalyst-to-process pathway changes it, and whether a backward-designed catalyst target is physically reachable. In ammonia synthesis, the atomic order Ru > Os > Fe becomes Fe > Ru > Os after candidate-specific process and economic optimization. Equalizing the Ru and Fe metal prices and fully reoptimizing the process reverses that order, with Ru reaching 14.712 versus 15.292 US dollars per tonne of NH3 for Fe, identifying metal cost coupled to process reoptimization as the causal boundary. Around the canonical economic regime, Fe nevertheless remains lower-cost in all 5,000 preregistered joint cost-uncertainty draws. Backward design places the canonical Ru activity-parity requirement at 201-fold, while even the 5th-percentile requirement under cost uncertainty is 70.78-fold, both far above the 2.525-fold maximum scaling-consistent headroom. In CO2-to-methanol, Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states reshape through a selectivity-recycle pathway, whereas a literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 control preserves the upstream order under a common monotonic mapping. A frozen decision-aware agent then operationalizes the same framework as a reusable execution layer: it recovers the complete industrial decision below a 206-CU deterministic-policy threshold, with a 22-CU protocol-complete oracle defining the scientific lower bound. By separating reaction-specific deterministic tools from decision-level action selection, the Agent layer converts a one-off multiscale analysis into a workflow that can be repeatedly executed over catalyst-screening tasks under explicit compute budgets. Together, these results connect ranking fidelity, catalyst-to-process coupling, physical reachability and scalable decision execution at the industrial frontier.

## Introduction

Computational catalyst discovery increasingly relies on adsorption energies, reactivity descriptors, scaling relations, volcano relations and surrogate models to rank large materials spaces.[1–6] Descriptor-based microkinetic frameworks have made these relations operational across broad catalyst families, while first-principles multiscale modelling has connected elementary-step energetics to experimentally relevant rates and conditions.[6,7] Earlier work linked first-principles ammonia descriptors directly to reactor conditions and catalyst selection,[32] and optimization frameworks formalized mechanistic modelling and catalyst design as coupled optimization problems.[33,36] Catalyst-testing studies have also shown that relative rankings can depend on the reactor or test environment; in fluid catalytic cracking, laboratory reactor choice can produce erroneous catalyst rankings relative to more representative operation.[35] The unresolved question addressed here is therefore narrower: when an atomistic screen is propagated through candidate-specific process optimization and economics, does the *ordering among discrete catalyst candidates* remain faithful to the industrial decision?

This question is most consequential near the decision frontier. Screening is comparative, and a model may remain well correlated across a broad candidate population while changing the identity of the leading candidate. Global predictive quality therefore does not guarantee fidelity where experimental and computational resources are concentrated. The same issue arises under uncertainty: adaptive-sampling and Bayesian-error approaches have shown that uncertainty should be interpreted relative to the quantity or decision being resolved, rather than treated as a uniform reason to refine every part of a model.[8–10]

We formulate this problem in two directions. Forward propagation maps catalyst properties through kinetics and process optimization to an industrial objective. Backward design starts from an economic target, identifies the catalyst-property improvement required to reach it, and tests whether that target lies within an admissible catalyst-property manifold. Integrated material-and-process design has previously shown how process objectives can be inverted into idealized molecular targets,[34] while catalyst-design frameworks have optimized descriptors directly inside kinetic models.[33,36] Our focus is candidate-level parity and reachability: which catalyst wins after industrial propagation, what property shift would be required to overturn that decision, and whether that shift is compatible with the catalyst manifold represented by the underlying scaling relations.

We apply the framework to two reaction systems and one rank-preservation control. Ammonia synthesis probes an activity-inventory/reactor-demand pathway across a 15-metal screen grounded in established scaling, Brønsted-Evans-Polanyi and volcano relations for transition-metal ammonia synthesis.[5,11,12] CO2-to-methanol probes a selectivity-recycle pathway using literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states together with an explicit process loop; this reaction class is strongly conditioned by conversion, selectivity, recycle and separation choices at process scale.[13–15] Au/TiO2 CO oxidation provides a counterfactual with a literature-calibrated monotonic size-activity mapping and a long-established sensitivity of low-temperature CO oxidation to supported Au particle size.[16–19]

Across the three catalytic systems, multiscale propagation spans a continuum from exact rank preservation to local reshuffling and decision-frontier inversion. The governing variable is the strength and topology of catalyst-specific coupling to downstream process decisions. We therefore separate three questions that are often conflated in screening studies: whether a ranking changes, which catalyst-to-process pathway causes the change, and whether an economically useful catalyst target lies inside the admissible material manifold. We then address a fourth methodological question: how this bidirectional analysis can be executed repeatedly rather than rebuilt as a one-off study. To do so, we wrap the deterministic scientific chain in a tool-using decision agent that selects admissible calculations under a finite compute budget while leaving the physics, process models and economic equations unchanged.[20–31,37,38] The resulting Agent layer is the automation and scaling mechanism of the workflow: reaction-specific tools provide the scientific content, while the policy allocates which parts of that content need to be evaluated for each decision instance.

## Results

### Industrial propagation can reverse a locally important catalyst ranking

The 15-metal ammonia screen places Ru, Os and Fe first, second and third in atomic activity (Fig. 1c), consistent with the established activity landscape for transition-metal ammonia synthesis.[11,12] After candidate-specific process optimization and evaluation of the frozen catalyst-dependent economic objective (Fig. 1a,d,e), the order becomes Fe, Ru and Os, with costs of 15.292, 22.031 and 25.832 US dollars per tonne of NH3, respectively (Fig. 1b,h).

The reversal is concentrated at the decision frontier rather than distributed across the full screen. For the three leading atomic candidates, Spearman rho is -0.50 and Kendall tau is -0.33; across all 15 metals, the raw Spearman correlation remains 0.929 (Fig. 1f,g). Thus a screen can preserve strong global structure while selecting a different leading candidate among the materials most likely to be pursued. Frontier-local rank fidelity, rather than full-set correlation alone, determines whether an atomistic ranking supports the downstream industrial choice.

### Price-process coupling defines the ammonia decision boundary

Candidate-specific process freedom places the leading metals in different operating regimes. Fe occupies an interior optimum near 425 °C and 180 bar with a 30 °C separator, whereas Ru prefers approximately 450 °C and 425 bar with a 25 °C separator; Os retains a broad shallow high-pressure minimum (Fig. 2b,c). The economic ranking is therefore evaluated after each catalyst has moved to its own preferred process state.

A direct price counterfactual identifies the causal boundary of the Fe-Ru inversion. Holding every non-price input, constraint and costing convention fixed, we set the Ru metal price equal to the Fe value of 8 US dollars per kilogram and reoptimized Ru over the full 14,136-state process library. The Ru optimum shifts from 450 °C and 425 bar to 425 °C and 170 bar, and the optimized cost falls to 14.712 US dollars per tonne of NH3, 0.580 US dollars per tonne below Fe (Fig. 2b–e). The baseline inversion therefore requires the Ru-Fe price disparity in the current model, but the response is not a static subtraction of metal inventory: changing the catalyst price also changes the preferred process regime. The supported mechanism is metal cost coupled to process reoptimization.

The canonical 6.739 US dollars per tonne Ru-Fe gap is distributed across several cost pools. Fresh-feed compression contributes +4.632 US dollars per tonne, metal inventory +1.763, compressor capital +1.181 and refrigeration +0.629, while vessel pressure, recycle compression and reactor-base terms offset part of the gap (Fig. 2a,e). The decomposition explains where the baseline difference resides; the fully reoptimized equal-price intervention shows how the coupled process responds when the price driver is changed.

Descriptor uncertainty and economic uncertainty act on different decision endpoints. Across 1,000 frozen descriptor-uncertainty draws, Fe is feasible under the 90 m3 bed-volume criterion in 79.9% of realizations, is the economic Top-1 candidate in 68.1% (681/1000), and remains within the actionable Top-3 in 94.0%. Atomic-to-economic Top-1 survival is 28.2%, a distinct statistic measuring preservation of the upstream winner rather than the probability that Fe ranks first (Fig. 2f,g).

The preregistered joint cost Monte Carlo is a local robustness test around the canonical economic regime rather than the large equal-price intervention above. Across 5,000 draws that jointly vary metal price, the CAPEX coefficient, electricity price and catalyst lifetime, full process reoptimization gives P(C_Fe < C_Ru) = 1.000, with the smallest sampled Ru-Fe cost gap equal to 2.382 US dollars per tonne (Fig. 2h). The corresponding Ru activity-parity distribution spans 70.78-fold, 174.27-fold and 462.00-fold at the 5th, 50th and 95th percentiles, respectively (Fig. 3b). The equal-price experiment therefore identifies a causal boundary, whereas the Monte Carlo quantifies robustness within the canonical neighborhood.

### Backward design separates economic requirement from physical reachability

Forward propagation identifies the preferred catalyst but does not reveal how far a losing candidate lies from parity. We therefore increased Ru intrinsic activity and reoptimized the full process at each multiplier. Under the canonical economic parameters, Ru reaches the Fe cost reference only after a 201.22-fold activity increase (Fig. 3a,c).

The corresponding scaling-consistent design space is much narrower. Accessible activity headroom is 1.090-fold at 673 K and reaches a maximum of 2.525-fold over the frozen process-state library. Under strict scaling, the minimum Ru cost remains 21.398 US dollars per tonne of NH3 at E_N = -1.215 eV, above Fe (Fig. 3d,e). The canonical 201.22-fold requirement is therefore a reference target for one economic regime, not a fixed material property. Across the tested cost-uncertainty envelope, even the 5th-percentile activity-parity requirement of 70.78-fold remains far outside the 2.525-fold maximum scaling-consistent headroom (Fig. 3b).

Backward design thus converts the industrial disadvantage into a distribution of catalyst requirements and tests those requirements against the admissible material manifold. For the present ammonia system, the activity-only route remains unreachable throughout the tested economic envelope, closing the case for incremental intrinsic-activity improvement along the current scaling path.

### Methanol transfer reveals a selectivity-recycle pathway

The framework was transferred to a distinct process architecture using four literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states (Fig. 4a,b).[13] With space-time yield per gram of Re as the upstream metric, the order is 1 wt% Re at 250 °C, 1 wt% Re at 200 °C, 5 wt% Re at 200 °C and 5 wt% Re at 250 °C. After propagation through the explicit recycle and separation model at the source-anchored 2% purge condition, the economic order becomes 5 wt% Re at 200 °C, 1 wt% Re at 200 °C, 1 wt% Re at 250 °C and 5 wt% Re at 250 °C, with net production costs of 943, 967, 975 and 1,258 euros per tonne, respectively (Fig. 4c).

The Re-normalized upstream winner falls from first to third. Across the four states, Spearman rho is 0.20, Kendall tau is 0.00 and three of six pairwise comparisons invert (Fig. 4e). Methane formation consumes reactants and increases gas accumulation, purge, recycle and compression burden. At the 5 wt% Re, 250 °C benchmark, local economic leverage is 0.37579 for methane suppression, 0.05883 for single-pass conversion and 0.00289 for space-time yield (Fig. 4a,g). The process sensitivity is consistent with the broader CO2-to-methanol literature, in which equilibrium-limited conversion, recycle architecture and separation burden materially affect plant-level performance.[14,15]

The reshuffling persists across the available loop degree of freedom. Sweeping purge from 0.5% to 40% over 396 levels leaves the Re-normalized upstream winner economically suboptimal at every purge fraction; rho never exceeds 0.40 and at least two of six pairs remain inverted. Candidate-specific purge optimization changes the detailed order but does not restore the upstream ranking (Fig. 4f).

The four-candidate economic order also remains unchanged in all 5,000 draws of the cost-parameter analysis (Fig. 4d). Because the canonical D01 objective excludes Re purchase and replacement, metal-price and lifetime perturbations are structurally inactive in that canonical calculation. A separately labelled active-Re replacement extension, using STY-derived Re inventory, likewise preserves the same four-state order in all 5,000 draws. The extension is a robustness analysis and does not redefine the canonical D01 economic objective.

### Coupling topology determines whether rankings reshape or survive

Ammonia and methanol expose distinct routes from catalyst properties to their system-specific downstream economic objectives (Fig. 5a). In ammonia, intrinsic activity and metal cost jointly determine catalyst inventory and the preferred operating regime, which then redistribute compression, reactor and equipment burdens. In methanol, selectivity controls reactant loss, gas accumulation, purge, recycle and compression. Absolute cost values are not compared across the two reactions; the transferable object is the topology linking catalyst properties to the cost pools that control each reaction-specific decision.

A literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 CO-oxidation control tests the complementary regime. Low-temperature CO oxidation on supported Au has long shown pronounced particle-size and support effects,[16,17] and the present control uses an absolute-rate anchor from Janssens et al.[18] together with the closest-loading size dependence from Overbury et al.[19] Candidate states differ only in Au particle size from 2 to 6 nm under a common operating condition and process topology.

Under this common monotonic mapping, intrinsic activity and required catalyst burden preserve the same order, 2 > 3 > 4 > 5 > 6 nm. Spearman rho and Kendall tau are both 1.000, no pairwise comparison inverts, and all 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws preserve the complete order (Fig. 5b–d). A semi-open extension allowing moderate candidate-specific kinetic and operating freedom preserves the exact order in 92.16% of 10,000 draws over 273.15–293.15 K, with mean rho = 0.99214. Extending the temperature window to 313.15 K lowers exact preservation to 72.62% while retaining mean rho = 0.96802 (Fig. 5e). The control therefore shows that multiscale propagation does not generate inversion by construction; ranking change depends on the strength and topology of candidate-specific downstream coupling (Fig. 5f).

### A decision-aware agent turns the framework into a reusable execution workflow

The bidirectional framework is scientifically useful as a single analysis, but its broader value depends on whether the same decision logic can be executed repeatedly across screening tasks without manually prescribing every calculation. DISCOVER V1 therefore exposes the deterministic chain through a frozen 11-action scientific interface. The language-model policy selects admissible actions under a finite compute budget, while all numerical physics and economics remain tool-executed (Fig. 6a). This separation turns the framework into a reusable execution workflow: reaction-specific models define the scientific state and admissible tools, whereas the Agent determines which calculations are needed to close each decision. The primary endpoint requires the correct economic winner, decision-pair ordering and reachability classification, while numerical recovery of the backward parity multiplier is scored separately.

The deterministic fixed policy completes at 206 compute units (CU). In the boundary study, the strong model reaches 20/20 complete decisions at a 75-CU allowance, with a median 52.5 CU to the ledger-true decision-stable point; at 50 CU, completion falls to 13/20 even though winner and decision-pair identification remain correct in all 20 runs (Fig. 6c,e). Neither weaker tier reaches this regime at the tested allowances. Their action errors concentrate in budget allocation and action sequencing, and a typed interface that removes the mini tier's interface errors moves its failures into those layers without restoring completion (Fig. 6c,g).

A deterministic oracle places these numbers on an absolute scale. The literal scorer-complete floor is 7 CU, but it can exploit incomplete screening. The protocol-complete S1-S3 oracle requires 22 CU and includes a process window, all-candidate activity screening, optimization of the minimum unresolved Fe/Ru/Os set, backward design and reachability. Relative to this scientific lower bound, the strong 75-CU allowance is 3.41 times oracle, its median 52.5-CU decision-stable spend is 2.39 times oracle, and the fixed-policy threshold is 9.36 times oracle (Fig. 6b,f).

Decision convergence precedes quantitative-target convergence. The canonical Ru-to-Fe parity multiplier is recovered in 9/20 runs at 75 CU and 20/20 at 225 CU. Thus a complete industrial decision can close before every intermediate numerical quantity converges, provided that the residual quantitative error does not cross the reachability boundary.

The below-threshold mechanism is adaptive scope allocation. At constrained budgets the strong tier repeatedly constructs and uses process windows smaller than the full 14,136-state domain. Under the non-binding 5,000-CU allowance, canonical narrow-window use is 0/20, median decision stabilization moves to 566 CU, and the policy spends a further median 148 CU before self-stop, producing 714 CU median final spend (Fig. 6d,e). Budget pressure therefore activates search compression; when that pressure disappears, the decision itself stabilizes much later before additional post-stability computation is consumed.

The Agent result therefore serves two roles. First, it defines the capability- and budget-dependent envelope in which selective computation closes the same scientific decision before deterministic full coverage is affordable. Second, it provides the workflow-level mechanism for scaling the analysis beyond a manually scripted one-off calculation: the deterministic modules remain responsible for scientific truth, while the Agent orchestrates their repeated use across decision instances. The present benchmark quantifies this orchestration on the frozen NH3 decision chain; the architecture is designed so that additional reaction systems enter through their own deterministic tool modules rather than by changing the decision logic itself.

## Discussion

The central result is that catalyst screening should be evaluated at the industrial decision frontier rather than only by global rank agreement. In ammonia, the full 15-metal ordering remains strongly correlated while the leading atomic candidates reorder after candidate-specific process and economic propagation. This local failure mode is consequential because experimental and computational effort is concentrated precisely where the ranking changes.

The Ru price counterfactual identifies the causal structure of that reversal. Equalizing the Ru and Fe metal prices flips the economic order, establishing that the canonical Fe-over-Ru result depends on the direct price disparity in the current model. Yet the same intervention also moves Ru from a high-pressure to a low-pressure optimum, redistributing compression and capital burdens. Catalyst economics and process optimization therefore form one coupled mechanism: price changes the preferred process state, and the process state determines how the price difference propagates into the final objective.

That causal intervention should be distinguished from local robustness. The equal-price test moves Ru far from its canonical price regime to expose the boundary at which the decision changes. The 5,000-draw economic Monte Carlo instead perturbs the canonical neighborhood and leaves Fe lower-cost in every draw. Descriptor uncertainty acts on yet another layer, giving a 68.1% probability that Fe is the economic Top-1 candidate while preserving Fe within the actionable Top-3 in 94.0% of draws. The useful uncertainty quantity is therefore the probability that propagated uncertainty changes feasibility, winner identity, rank order or reachability, not simply the variance of an upstream parameter.

Backward design converts the downstream decision into a catalyst requirement. The canonical 201.22-fold Ru activity target is one economic reference, while the joint cost analysis broadens the target to a 70.78-462.00-fold central 90% interval. The important conclusion is invariant across that range: even the 5th-percentile target exceeds the 2.525-fold maximum scaling-consistent headroom by more than an order of magnitude. The activity-only route is therefore closed throughout the tested economic envelope, redirecting design effort toward changes that alter the accessible material class, selectivity, stability or process architecture.

The cross-reaction comparison clarifies what transfers. NH3 and MeOH use different frozen downstream economic objectives, so their absolute cost values are not compared. What is transferred is the decision logic and the coupling topology. In ammonia, activity and metal cost couple to inventory and the preferred operating regime; in methanol, selectivity couples to feed loss, purge, recycle and compression. The Au/TiO2 control shows the complementary case in which a common monotonic mapping preserves the upstream order. Ranking preservation, reshaping and inversion can therefore be understood as different regimes of catalyst-to-process coupling rather than as a universal consequence of adding model layers.

The Agent contribution is the scaling layer of the framework rather than merely an efficiency add-on. Tool-using language models, autonomous chemistry systems and adaptive experiment-selection methods establish the broader context for action selection under evolving scientific evidence.[20–31] Here the 22-CU protocol-complete oracle defines the irreducible scientific chain, while the strong policy reaches a median decision-stable point at 52.5 CU in the 75-CU cell, compared with the 206-CU deterministic threshold. Under a non-binding allowance, narrow-window allocation disappears and decision stabilization moves to 566 CU before another 148 CU is spent. The mechanism is therefore budget-induced search compression. More importantly, separating policy from deterministic scientific tools creates a reusable execution pattern: once a reaction-specific model exposes the same decision objects—ranking, feasibility, parity and reachability—the workflow can be applied repeatedly across candidate sets without hard-coding a full calculation sequence for every case.

Taken together, the results define a decision-centered workflow that is both scientific and executable at scale. Forward propagation tests whether an upstream ranking survives a system-specific industrial objective. Counterfactual interventions identify which catalyst and economic variables generate the decision boundary. Backward design maps that boundary to a catalyst target and tests physical reachability. The Agent layer then turns these deterministic steps into a reusable decision engine that can repeatedly prioritize the calculations still capable of changing the industrial endpoint. In this form, the framework is not limited to explaining individual ranking inversions; it provides a route to batch, decision-aware catalyst screening as additional reaction-specific tool modules are connected.

## Methods

### Multiscale decision analysis

Each catalytic system is propagated through its own frozen downstream objective. The common quantity compared across systems is **ranking behavior and catalyst-to-process coupling**, not the absolute numerical value of the economic objective. NH3 uses the frozen catalyst-dependent cost objective of NH3-FINAL-1.1; MEOH-D01-v3 uses its near-full-plant net production cost; the Au/TiO2 control uses required catalyst burden under a common process mapping. Absolute costs are therefore interpreted only within each reaction system.

Within a system, rankings before and after propagation are compared using Spearman and Kendall correlations, pairwise inversions and top-rank identity. Frontier analyses focus on leading upstream candidates while full-set statistics distinguish local decision changes from global rank disorder. Backward design varies a selected catalyst property, reoptimizes the downstream process and identifies the value required for economic parity, which is then compared with the reachable catalyst-property or scaling manifold. Causal counterfactuals change one predefined driver while preserving all other model conventions and rerunning the same downstream optimization.

### Ammonia synthesis

The ammonia analysis uses NH3-FINAL-1.1. The 15-metal screen is propagated through frozen scaling and kinetic relations, catalyst productivity and inventory, candidate-specific process-state optimization, pressure-dependent equipment capital and seven catalyst-dependent economic cost pools. Temperature, pressure and separator temperature are reoptimized per candidate over the full 14,136-state process library. Descriptor uncertainty is propagated through 1,000 frozen draws; Fe feasibility is evaluated against the 90 m3 bed-volume criterion, and economic-winner, Top-1-survival and Top-3-actionable outcomes are retained separately.

The Ru price counterfactual changes only the Ru metal purchase price, setting it equal to the Fe value of 8 US dollars per kilogram, while all kinetic, engineering, costing and optimization conventions remain unchanged; Ru is then fully reoptimized over the same process library. The canonical Fe-Ru cost gap is decomposed over the same seven cost pools.

The preregistered cost-side Monte Carlo uses 5,000 draws with seed 20260920. Fe and Ru metal-price multipliers are sampled independently from a log-uniform distribution over 0.5-2.0. A common CAPEX multiplier is sampled uniformly over 0.8-1.2, electricity price uniformly over 20-100 US dollars per megawatt-hour, and catalyst lifetime uniformly over 5-20 years. Fe and Ru are reoptimized for every draw. The Ru activity-parity multiplier alpha* is recomputed against the draw-specific reoptimized Fe target.

The canonical Ru backward-design calculation varies intrinsic activity with full downstream reoptimization, and reachability is evaluated over the frozen descriptor path and process-state library. The mechanistic and scaling basis follows the established transition-metal ammonia-synthesis literature.[5,11,12]

### CO2-to-methanol

The methanol analysis uses the restored D01 v3 recycle/separation model and four Re/TiO2 states derived from the 1 and 5 wt% catalysts reported by Gothe et al.[13] The canonical comparison uses 2% purge. Upstream rankings are reconstructed from space-time yield per gram of Re, single-pass methanol yield and space-time yield per gram of catalyst; downstream ranking uses the frozen near-full-plant net production cost. Re purchase price and catalyst replacement are excluded from the canonical objective. Purge robustness spans 0.5-40% over 396 levels, with additional candidate-specific purge reoptimization. Local leverage for space-time yield, conversion and methane suppression is evaluated at the 5 wt% Re, 250 °C benchmark. The explicit-loop interpretation is consistent with established CO2-to-methanol process assessments in which recycle, compression and separation contribute materially to plant performance.[14,15]

The 5,000-draw economic-parameter analysis propagates the sampled CAPEX and electricity terms through the candidate-specific D01 accounting while retaining the canonical objective definition. Because Re purchase and replacement are absent from that boundary, sampled metal-price and lifetime variables are structurally inactive in the canonical calculation. A separately labelled extension adds active-Re replacement using STY-derived Re inventory and the frozen Re price, with the same sampled price and lifetime multipliers; this extension is reported as a robustness analysis and does not replace MEOH-D01-v3.

### Au/TiO2 rank-preservation control

The canonical control compares 2–6 nm Au/TiO2 states under a common fixed condition and process topology. The reference rate, Au loading and dispersion are anchored to Janssens et al.,[18] and the nominal size exponent to the 4.5 wt% Au/TiO2 series of Overbury et al.[19] The broader physical basis for low-temperature supported-Au activity and size sensitivity follows the established Au/TiO2 literature.[16,17] Rank preservation is evaluated in 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws. A semi-open extension perturbs candidate kinetics and independently optimizes temperature and O2/CO ratio within literature-constrained windows.

### Decision-aware workflow automation and agent benchmark

DISCOVER V1 is evaluated as the workflow-automation layer over the frozen deterministic decision chain. It exposes an 11-action interface; the language-model policy receives a finite CU budget and selects admissible actions, while the environment executes all numerical work. The Agent does not alter the physical models, process constraints, economic equations or ground-truth decision geometry. This separation is the basis for repeated deployment: scientific modules can be replaced or extended reaction by reaction while the decision layer continues to operate on the same classes of evidence—ranking, feasibility, parity and reachability. The frozen primary endpoint is `full_decision_correct = winner_correct ∧ pair_decision_correct ∧ reachability_correct`; backward parity-multiplier accuracy is scored independently.

The deterministic fixed-policy reference completes at 206 CU. Cross-model tests use the frozen anonymous closed-book task. DISCOVER-BOUNDARY-C1 probes the strong tier around the fixed-policy boundary, weaker-tier transfer cells and a non-binding 5,000-CU allowance. Narrow-window allocation requires a process window smaller than the 14,136-state domain followed by a successful scoped action using that window. Decision-stable CU is the cumulative ledger spend from which the complete endpoint remains correct through the final action.

Two deterministic oracle lower bounds are retained. The 7-CU scorer-complete floor is the shortest legal action chain that yields a scorer-correct winner, decision pair and reachability classification when the oracle may select only decision-relevant candidates. The manuscript-facing protocol-complete oracle additionally satisfies the S1-S3 scientific stopping criteria: it constructs a valid process window, computes activity for all 15 candidates, resolves the minimum Fe/Ru/Os process set, performs backward design and closes reachability. The resulting minimum is 22 CU. All Agent headline values are reproduced from retained raw traces with the frozen scorer and ledger.

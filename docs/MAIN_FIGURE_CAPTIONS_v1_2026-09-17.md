## Figure 1 | Industrial propagation reverses the ammonia decision frontier

**a,** Atomic activity ranking of 15 candidate metals compared with the catalyst-dependent economic ranking after candidate-specific process optimization. Ru, Os and Fe occupy the first three positions in the atomic screen, whereas Fe, Ru and Os are the three lowest-cost candidates after propagation, with costs of 15.292, 22.031 and 25.832 US dollars per tonne of NH3, respectively. **b,** Spearman rank correlation as the upstream candidate set is expanded from the decision frontier to the full screen. The top-three correlation is -0.50, while the full-15 correlation is 0.929. The contrast localizes the ranking reversal to the high-value decision frontier despite strong global rank agreement.

## Figure 2 | Process reoptimization and uncertainty define the actionable ammonia region

**a,** Optimized catalyst-dependent cost envelopes for Fe, Ru and Os after inclusion of pressure-dependent equipment capital and reoptimization of reactor temperature and separator temperature at each pressure. Fe occupies an interior optimum near 425 °C and 180 bar, Ru near 450 °C and 425 bar, and Os retains a broad shallow high-pressure minimum. **b,** Propagation of descriptor uncertainty through the frozen NH3-FINAL-1.1 model over 1,000 draws. Fe satisfies the 90 m3 bed-volume criterion in 79.9% of realizations, remains the economic Top-1 candidate in 28.2%, and remains within the actionable Top-3 in 94.0%. The distributions show that uncertainty is filtered by kinetic, feasibility and process constraints before reaching the final decision.

## Figure 3 | Backward design separates economic requirement from scaling-consistent reachability

**a,** Reoptimized Ru catalyst-dependent cost as a function of an imposed activity multiplier. Cost parity with Fe requires an approximately 201-fold increase in Ru activity. **b,** Comparison of this backward-designed economic requirement with the activity gain accessible along the frozen scaling-consistent descriptor manifold. Reachable activity headroom is 1.090-fold at 673 K and reaches a maximum of 2.525-fold over the admissible process-state library. The strict-scaling Ru minimum is 21.398 US dollars per tonne of NH3 at E_N = -1.215 eV, above the Fe reference. The activity-only parity target therefore lies outside the current scaling-consistent design path.

## Figure 4 | Methanol ranking reshaping follows a selectivity-recycle pathway

**a,** Upstream and downstream rankings of four literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states. Using space-time yield per gram of Re, 1 wt% Re at 250 °C ranks first upstream but falls to third after propagation through the explicit recycle/separation model at the canonical 2% purge; 5 wt% Re at 200 °C becomes the economic winner. The resulting economic costs are 943, 967, 975 and 1,258 euros per tonne in economic-rank order, with Spearman rho = 0.20, Kendall tau = 0.00 and three of six pairwise inversions. **b,** Net production cost across the complete 0.5-40% purge sweep (396 levels). **c,** Local economic leverage at 5 wt% Re and 250 °C for space-time yield (0.00289), single-pass conversion (0.05883) and methane suppression (0.37579). The dominant methane-associated leverage identifies the selectivity-recycle pathway underlying the rank reshaping.

## Figure 5 | Catalyst-to-process coupling determines whether rankings invert or survive

**a,** Reaction-specific propagation pathways resolved by the framework. In ammonia, catalyst activity acts primarily through catalyst inventory, reactor demand and process severity; in CO2-to-methanol, selectivity acts through reactant loss, gas accumulation, purge, recycle and compression. **b,** Literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 CO-oxidation control across 2-6 nm Au particle sizes under the common canonical process condition. Intrinsic activity and downstream catalyst burden preserve the same order, 2 > 3 > 4 > 5 > 6 nm, with Spearman rho = 1.000, Kendall tau = 1.000, zero pairwise inversions and complete preservation in 10,000 of 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws. **c,** Semi-open robustness extension. Allowing candidate-specific kinetic perturbations and operating-condition optimization retains mean rho = 0.99214 over 273.15-293.15 K and 0.96802 over the wider 273.15-313.15 K sensitivity range, while permitting occasional local reshuffling.

## Figure 6 | Decision-aware scientific compute has a capability-bounded operating envelope

**a,** Complete-decision recovery in the frozen anonymous DISCOVER benchmark and boundary extension. The primary endpoint requires the correct economic winner, decision pair and reachability classification. Original cross-model recovery is 6/35 for nano, 15/35 for mini and 35/35 for the strong model. The deterministic fixed policy completes at 206 compute units (CU); the strong model reaches 20/20 complete decisions at 75 CU and falls to 13/20 at 50 CU. **b,** Quantitative backward-target recovery converges later: the canonical Ru-to-Fe parity multiplier is recovered in 9/20 runs at 75 CU and 20/20 at 225 CU. **c,** Canonical narrow-window allocation, defined as a process window smaller than the full 14,136-state domain followed by a successful scoped action, occurs in all strong-model runs from 50 to 175 CU, falls to 1/8 at 200 CU and is absent at 225 and 250 CU; neither weaker tier uses the mechanism at 175 CU. **d,** Decision-stable and final-used CU. Under a non-binding 5,000-CU allowance, all 20 strong-model runs recover the same audited decision, but median final spend rises to 714 CU and a median 148 CU is consumed after the decision has stabilized. The adaptive advantage is therefore localized to a constrained budget regime and depends on model capability.

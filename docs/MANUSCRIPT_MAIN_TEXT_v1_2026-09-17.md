# Industrial objectives reshape catalyst rankings across scales

## Abstract

Catalyst discovery is usually organized around atomistic descriptors, intrinsic rates and other upstream performance metrics, whereas industrial selection is made after kinetics, catalyst inventory, reactor design, separations, recycle and equipment costs have interacted. Here we connect these scales in a forward-and-backward framework that asks whether an upstream catalyst ranking survives industrial propagation, which process pathway drives any change, and whether an economically required catalyst improvement is physically reachable. In ammonia synthesis, the atomic top-three order Ru > Os > Fe becomes Fe > Ru > Os after candidate-specific process and economic optimization, even though the full 15-metal ranking remains strongly correlated. Backward design shows that Ru would require an approximately 201-fold activity increase to reach Fe cost parity, while the scaling-consistent activity headroom is at most 2.525-fold. In CO2-to-methanol, the ranking of Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states is reshaped through a selectivity-recycle pathway dominated by methane-associated feed and recycle penalties. A literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 control preserves the upstream order, establishing that multiscale propagation can preserve, weakly reshape or invert rankings depending on the coupling between catalyst properties and downstream process variables. A frozen decision-aware agent benchmark further shows that scientific compute can be reallocated to recover the industrial decision below a deterministic-policy budget threshold, with a capability- and budget-dependent operating envelope. These results recast catalyst screening as a decision problem across scales and provide a route from industrial objectives back to physically testable catalyst targets.

## Introduction

Catalyst screening reduces a large chemical design space to a tractable set of candidates by ranking materials according to descriptors, adsorption energies, intrinsic turnover rates, selectivity metrics or surrogate models derived from them. These quantities are indispensable for understanding catalytic chemistry, yet they are only upstream representations of an industrial decision. A commercial process selects a catalyst after reaction kinetics have propagated into catalyst inventory, reactor size, operating pressure and temperature, recycle, separations, replacement requirements and capital and operating costs. The ranking that matters at this final stage can therefore differ from the ranking that initiated the screen.

Multiscale catalyst and process models have traditionally been used to predict performance at increasing levels of physical detail. A distinct question is whether the ordering of candidates is conserved across those levels. Rank conservation matters because screening is fundamentally comparative: a model can predict each candidate accurately enough in isolation while still misidentifying the best candidate if downstream coupling changes their relative burden. This issue becomes most consequential near the decision frontier, where a small set of leading candidates receives most of the experimental and computational attention.

We formulate catalyst screening as a bidirectional decision problem. Forward propagation maps catalyst properties through kinetics and process optimization to an industrial objective. Backward design starts from an economic target and determines the catalyst-property improvement required to reach it, followed by a test of whether that target lies on an admissible catalyst-property manifold. This structure separates three questions that are often conflated: whether a ranking changes, which catalyst-to-process pathway causes the change, and whether the improvement suggested by economics is physically reachable.

We apply the framework to two chemically and process-distinct cases and one rank-preservation control. Ammonia synthesis tests an activity-inventory/reactor-demand pathway across a 15-metal catalyst screen with candidate-specific process reoptimization. CO2-to-methanol tests a selectivity-recycle pathway using four literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states and an explicit recycle/separation loop. A literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 CO-oxidation system provides a counterfactual in which the downstream mapping remains monotonic. We then place the same decision chain inside a frozen scientific-action environment to test whether an AI agent can allocate finite computational resources to the parts of the chain that determine the industrial decision.

Across these cases, the effect of multiscale propagation is conditional. Ammonia shows a concentrated inversion at the decision frontier, methanol shows a selectivity-driven reshaping whose top-rank reversal depends on the upstream screening metric, and the Au/TiO2 control preserves the ranking under the canonical mapping. The agent benchmark adds a second layer to this result: once the scientific objective is defined at the industrial decision level, the value of additional computation depends on where uncertainty remains in the decision chain and on whether the model can direct computation to that location.

## Results

### Industrial propagation reverses the ammonia decision frontier

We first asked whether the atomic activity ranking of ammonia-synthesis catalysts survives propagation through process optimization and catalyst-dependent economics. The 15-metal atomic screen places Ru, Os and Fe first, second and third. After candidate-specific optimization of the process state and evaluation of the catalyst-dependent economic objective, the top-three order becomes Fe, Ru and Os, with costs of 15.292, 22.031 and 25.832 US dollars per tonne of NH3, respectively (Fig. 1a).

The reversal is concentrated among the candidates that matter most to the decision. The Spearman correlation for the top three candidates is -0.50, whereas the correlation across the full 15-metal set remains 0.929 (Fig. 1b). Thus a globally well-preserved ranking can still produce a different industrial winner. The relevant failure mode is local: the upstream ordering remains broadly informative over the full screen while losing decision fidelity at the frontier.

Process reoptimization does not remove this result. With pressure-dependent equipment capital included, Fe occupies an interior low-pressure optimum near 425 °C and 180 bar, Ru moves to an interior high-pressure optimum near 450 °C and 425 bar, and Os exhibits a broad shallow high-pressure minimum. The optimized operating regimes therefore differ across catalysts, but the economic ordering remains Fe > Ru > Os (Fig. 2a).

Atomistic uncertainty also propagates non-uniformly through the multiscale chain. In 1,000 uncertainty draws, Fe is feasible under the specified bed-volume constraint in 79.9% of realizations, remains top-ranked in 28.2%, and remains within the actionable top three in 94.0% (Fig. 2b). The same energetic perturbation can be amplified by kinetics, truncated by a feasibility boundary or absorbed by process reoptimization. Industrial decision sensitivity therefore depends on where the uncertainty sits relative to the active constraints of the process.

### Backward design separates an economic target from a reachable catalyst target

Forward propagation identifies Fe as the preferred ammonia catalyst under the current model, but it does not by itself quantify what Ru would need to change. We therefore inverted the problem. Ru activity was increased while the full process was reoptimized at each multiplier until the optimized Ru cost reached Fe parity. The required activity increase is approximately 201-fold (Fig. 3a).

We then compared this economic requirement with the activity gain available along the frozen scaling-consistent catalyst-property manifold. The accessible headroom is only 1.090-fold at 673 K and reaches a maximum of 2.525-fold over the admissible process-state library. The strict-scaling Ru minimum remains 21.398 US dollars per tonne of NH3 at E_N = -1.215 eV, above the Fe reference (Fig. 3b). The activity-only route to parity therefore lies far outside the current scaling-consistent design space.

This backward calculation changes the interpretation of a catalyst target. The economically relevant question is no longer whether Ru can be made more active in a generic sense; it is whether a catalyst modification can close a quantified process-level gap while remaining compatible with the physically admissible property manifold. In the present ammonia case, activity improvement alone does not provide that route.

### Methanol ranking reshaping follows a selectivity-recycle pathway

To test whether the same phenomenon transfers across a different process architecture, we reconstructed a literature-derived CO2-to-methanol case containing four Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states. Using space-time yield per gram of Re as the upstream productivity metric, the order is 1 wt% Re at 250 °C, 1 wt% Re at 200 °C, 5 wt% Re at 200 °C and 5 wt% Re at 250 °C. After propagation through the explicit recycle and separation model at the source-anchored 2% purge condition, the economic order becomes 5 wt% Re at 200 °C, 1 wt% Re at 200 °C, 1 wt% Re at 250 °C and 5 wt% Re at 250 °C, with net production costs of 943, 967, 975 and 1,258 euros per tonne, respectively (Fig. 4a).

The upstream per-Re winner consequently falls from first to third. Across the four states, Spearman rho is 0.20, Kendall tau is 0.00, and three of six pairwise comparisons invert. This reshaping is tied to the downstream process pathway. Methane formation consumes reactants, increases gas accumulation and changes purge, recycle and compression burdens. At the 5 wt% Re, 250 °C benchmark, the local economic leverage associated with methane suppression is 0.37579, compared with 0.05883 for single-pass conversion and 0.00289 for space-time yield (Fig. 4b).

The loop degree of freedom does not eliminate the effect. Sweeping purge from 0.5% to 40% across 396 levels leaves the Re-normalized intrinsic winner economically suboptimal at every purge fraction; Spearman rho never exceeds 0.40 and at least two of six pairs remain inverted. Candidate-specific purge optimization gives a different detailed economic ordering, but the frontier remains reshaped and every optimum lies at the 0.5% lower boundary of the sweep.

The upstream normalization determines which state appears to be mis-ranked at the frontier. Space-time yield per gram of Re places 1 wt% Re at 250 °C first and produces a top-rank reversal. Single-pass methanol yield and space-time yield per gram of total catalyst place 5 wt% Re at 200 °C first, coinciding with the economic winner, while the global rank statistics remain rho = 0.20, tau = 0.00 and three of six inverted pairs. The process therefore reshapes the ordering under all three admissible screening metrics, while the identity of the apparent upstream winner depends on the scientific objective used for screening.

### Ranking behavior is set by catalyst-to-process coupling topology

The ammonia and methanol cases identify different routes from catalyst properties to industrial cost. In ammonia, activity primarily changes catalyst inventory, reactor demand and the preferred operating state. In methanol, selectivity changes reactant loss, gas accumulation, purge, recycle and compression. Their common feature is a candidate-dependent downstream mapping strong enough to alter relative burden near the decision frontier (Fig. 5a).

We tested the converse using a literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 CO-oxidation control. Candidate states differ in Au particle size from 2 to 6 nm while the active element, support, feed composition, temperature, pressure and process topology are held common. Under this canonical mapping, the intrinsic activity order and the required catalyst-burden order are identical: 2 > 3 > 4 > 5 > 6 nm. Spearman rho and Kendall tau are both 1.000, all pairwise comparisons are preserved, and 10,000 of 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws retain the complete ordering (Fig. 5b).

A semi-open extension allows candidate-specific kinetic perturbations and optimization over a literature-constrained temperature and feed-ratio window. In the primary 273.15-293.15 K range, the exact full order is preserved in 92.16% of 10,000 draws and the mean Spearman correlation is 0.99214. Extending the temperature range to 313.15 K reduces exact preservation to 72.62% while retaining a mean correlation of 0.96802. Moderate operating freedom therefore introduces local reshuffling without destroying the overall rank structure.

Together, the three systems define a continuum of rank behavior. A monotonic downstream mapping preserves the screen. Moderate candidate-specific freedom weakens exact preservation while maintaining strong global correlation. Strong catalyst-specific coupling to process variables can alter the decision frontier or the preferred candidate. The relevant descriptor of multiscale screening is therefore the topology and strength of the coupling between catalyst properties, the chosen upstream objective and downstream decision variables.

### Decision-aware compute allocation has a bounded operating envelope

The same framework also defines where scientific computation has decision value. We constructed DISCOVER V1 as a frozen environment in which an agent selects among admissible scientific actions under a finite compute budget. The deterministic physical and economic models remain fixed; the agent chooses which evidence to acquire and when to stop. The primary endpoint is complete recovery of the economic winner, the decision-pair ordering and the reachability classification. Numerical recovery of the backward parity multiplier is evaluated separately.

Across the original anonymous closed-book cross-model benchmark, complete-decision recovery is 6/35 for the nano tier, 15/35 for the mini tier and 35/35 for the strong tier (Fig. 6a). This tier dependence motivated a boundary analysis around the deterministic fixed-policy reference, which completes at 206 compute units (CU). The strong model achieves 20/20 complete decisions at 75 CU, with a median of 52 CU spent before the decision becomes stable. At 50 CU, completion falls to 13/20 even though the winner and decision pair remain correct in all 20 runs; the remaining failures occur when the closing reachability action no longer fits within the budget.

Decision convergence precedes quantitative-target convergence. Under the frozen first-record convention, the canonical Ru-to-Fe parity multiplier is recovered in 9/20 runs at 75 CU and rises to 20/20 at 225 CU. Thus 75 CU is the lowest tested budget with stable 20/20 complete-decision recovery, whereas 225 CU is the lowest tested budget with 20/20 recovery of the canonical quantitative target (Fig. 6b). A decision can therefore close before every intermediate numerical quantity has converged to its canonical value, provided that the remaining numerical uncertainty does not cross the final reachability boundary.

The mechanism is adaptive scope allocation. Below the fixed-policy threshold, the strong model repeatedly constructs process windows smaller than the full 14,136-state domain and uses those windows for subsequent scoped actions. Canonical narrow-window allocation occurs in all strong-model runs from 50 to 175 CU, falls to 1/8 at 200 CU and disappears at 225 and 250 CU. The weaker tiers do not reproduce this behavior at 175 CU. A typed and prevalidated tool interface removes undeclared-argument errors for the mini tier and improves winner identification, yet complete decisions remain 0/20 at 175 CU. Increasing the mini budget to 225, 300 and 400 CU produces only 6/20, 4/20 and 7/20 complete decisions, respectively.

The upper edge of the operating envelope appears when the budget ceases to constrain the policy. Under a non-binding 5,000-CU allowance, all 20 strong-model runs reach the same audited decision, yet median final spend rises to 714 CU and a median 148 CU is consumed after the decision has already become stable. Adaptive allocation therefore provides its clearest advantage in the constrained regime where selective computation can close a decision that full deterministic coverage cannot yet afford. The benefit is jointly conditioned on budget and model capability.

## Discussion

These results place the screening objective at the industrial decision frontier. Atomistic activity, selectivity and descriptor values remain essential scientific variables, but their value for candidate selection is mediated by the process pathways they control. A catalyst property with a large intrinsic effect can have weak economic leverage when downstream constraints absorb that change. A property with a modest intrinsic perturbation can dominate the decision when it changes recycle, separation, equipment or feasibility burden. Screening objectives should therefore be chosen with explicit knowledge of the downstream pathway linking the measured property to the industrial target.

The distinction between global rank correlation and decision-frontier fidelity is especially important for high-throughput discovery. In the ammonia screen, the full 15-metal correlation remains high while the top-three order reverses. A screening model can therefore appear statistically successful over the full candidate population and still allocate experimental effort to the wrong leading candidate. Reporting local rank behavior near the actionable frontier provides a more direct measure of decision quality than a single global correlation coefficient.

Backward design adds a second constraint to this logic. Industrial economics can define a quantitative catalyst target, but the target is scientifically useful only when compared with the catalyst-property manifold available to the material class and reaction mechanism. The 201-fold Ru activity requirement and 2.525-fold maximum scaling-consistent headroom illustrate this separation. The gap redirects design away from incremental activity optimization and toward other catalyst properties, alternative process architectures or different material families that can change the governing downstream pathway.

The cross-reaction comparison also argues against universal catalyst-screening heuristics. Ammonia is governed here by an activity-inventory/reactor-demand route, whereas the methanol case is governed by selectivity-sensitive recycle economics. The appropriate upstream metric therefore depends on the process architecture and on the denominator used to express catalytic performance. Reaction transfer should begin by identifying the catalyst-to-process coupling topology, then selecting descriptors and experiments that interrogate that topology directly.

The rank-preservation control establishes the complementary regime. Multiscale propagation can preserve a ranking when candidate differences enter a common monotonic downstream mapping. Partial relaxation of the operating point introduces local reorderings while leaving the rank structure strongly correlated. This provides a practical criterion for deciding when a simple upstream screen may be sufficient and when explicit process propagation is required: the need for multiscale decision analysis rises with the strength of candidate-specific downstream coupling near the frontier.

The agent benchmark extends the same principle from scientific variables to scientific computation. Compute has value when it resolves the part of the chain that can still change the decision. Under a binding budget, the strong model narrows the process domain and redirects resources toward the unresolved backward and reachability links, allowing the industrial decision to close below the deterministic-policy threshold. The effect disappears when the budget is loose and is absent in weaker model tiers. Agent performance in scientific workflows is therefore best characterized by an operating envelope over model capability, available compute and decision state, rather than by a single claim of generic efficiency.

Taken together, the framework connects catalyst discovery to industrial choice in both directions. Forward propagation tests whether a scientific ranking survives contact with process and economic constraints. Backward propagation translates the resulting industrial gap into a catalyst-property requirement and tests its reachability. Decision-aware allocation then identifies where additional computation is worth spending. This combination turns catalyst screening from a sequence of loosely connected models into a decision-centered design problem across scales.

## Methods

### Multiscale decision framework

Each candidate is propagated through a frozen sequence linking catalyst-level inputs to a process-level economic objective. Candidate rankings are compared before and after propagation using Spearman and Kendall rank correlations, pairwise inversion counts and changes in the top-ranked candidate. Decision-frontier analyses are evaluated on the leading upstream candidates, while full-set statistics are retained to distinguish local frontier changes from global rank disorder.

Backward design inverts the forward model with respect to a selected catalyst property. The property is varied, the downstream process is reoptimized, and the value required to reach economic parity with the reference candidate is identified. The resulting target is then compared with the reachable range generated by the frozen catalyst-property or scaling manifold.

### Ammonia synthesis model

The ammonia analysis uses the NH3-FINAL-1.1 model. The 15-metal catalyst screen is propagated through scaling and kinetic relations, catalyst productivity and inventory, candidate-specific reactor and process-state optimization, pressure-dependent equipment capital and catalyst-dependent economic terms. Temperature, pressure and separator temperature are reoptimized per candidate. The reported reduced catalyst-dependent economic objective contains the seven frozen catalyst-dependent cost pools used consistently across candidates.

Uncertainty is propagated by 1,000 draws through the frozen descriptor-to-process model. Fe feasibility is evaluated against the 90 m3 bed-volume criterion, and rank stability is summarized by top-one survival and actionable top-three frequency. The Ru backward-design calculation varies the Ru activity multiplier with full downstream reoptimization. Scaling-manifold reachability is evaluated over the frozen descriptor path and process-state library.

### CO2-to-methanol model

The methanol analysis uses the restored D01 v3 explicit recycle/separation model and four literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states. The canonical comparison is made at 2% purge. Upstream rankings are reconstructed using space-time yield per gram of Re, single-pass methanol yield and space-time yield per gram of total catalyst. Downstream ranking uses the frozen near-full-plant net production cost. Re purchase price is excluded from that objective by design.

Purge robustness is evaluated over the complete 0.5-40% sweep containing 396 levels. Purge is also reoptimized independently for each candidate state. Local leverage values for space-time yield, conversion and methane suppression are evaluated at the 5 wt% Re, 250 °C benchmark using the frozen D01 v3 mapping.

### Au/TiO2 rank-preservation control

The canonical control compares Au/TiO2 particle-size states from 2 to 6 nm under a common fixed operating condition and process topology. Literature calibration uses the retained experimental rate, loading and dispersion anchors and the frozen particle-size dependence. Rank preservation is evaluated in 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws. A semi-open extension independently perturbs candidate kinetics and allows optimization over literature-constrained temperature and O2/CO-ratio windows; the primary and wider temperature windows are analysed separately.

### Decision-aware agent benchmark

DISCOVER V1 exposes a frozen 11-action scientific interface over the same decision chain. The agent receives a finite compute budget and selects admissible actions; the environment executes all numerical work. The primary endpoint is `winner_correct ∧ pair_decision_correct ∧ reachability_correct`. Backward parity-multiplier accuracy is scored independently as a quantitative endpoint. The deterministic fixed-policy reference completes at 206 CU.

Cross-model tests use the frozen anonymous closed-book task and repeated sampling for the nano, mini and strong model tiers. DISCOVER-BOUNDARY-C1 probes the strong model around the deterministic threshold, including the 50-CU affordability floor, 75-CU decision-recovery boundary, 225-CU quantitative-target boundary and a non-binding 5,000-CU control. Narrow-window allocation requires a process window smaller than the full 14,136-state domain followed by a successful scoped action using that window. All reported headline values are replayable from the retained raw traces with the frozen scorer; provenance hashes and scorer-replay checks are executed from a clean repository checkout.

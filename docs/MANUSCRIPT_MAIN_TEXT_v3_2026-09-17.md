# Industrial objectives reshape catalyst rankings across scales

## Abstract

Catalyst discovery is commonly organized around atomistic descriptors and intrinsic rates, whereas industrial selection is made after those properties have propagated through kinetics, catalyst inventory, reactor operation, recycle, separation and cost. Here we formulate catalyst screening as a bidirectional decision problem that tests whether an upstream ranking survives industrial propagation and, when it does not, identifies the controlling process pathway and the catalyst improvement required to close the economic gap. In ammonia synthesis, the atomic order Ru > Os > Fe becomes Fe > Ru > Os after candidate-specific process and economic optimization, despite a high full-set rank correlation. Backward design shows that Ru requires an approximately 201-fold activity increase to reach Fe cost parity, compared with a maximum scaling-consistent headroom of 2.525-fold. In CO2-to-methanol, Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states are reshaped through a selectivity-recycle pathway dominated by methane-associated feed and recycle penalties. A literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 control preserves the upstream order, showing that rank change depends on catalyst-to-process coupling rather than on multiscale propagation itself. A frozen decision-aware agent benchmark further shows that the industrial decision can be recovered below a deterministic-policy compute threshold within a capability- and budget-dependent operating envelope. The framework links catalyst screening to industrial choice in both directions and converts process-level objectives into physically testable catalyst targets.

## Introduction

Computational catalyst discovery increasingly relies on adsorption energies, reactivity descriptors, scaling relations, volcano relations and surrogate models to rank large materials spaces.[1–6] Descriptor-based microkinetic frameworks have made these relations operational across broad catalyst families, while first-principles multiscale modelling has connected elementary-step energetics to experimentally relevant rates and conditions.[6,7] More recent work has shown that uncertainty-aware model refinement can focus expensive calculations on the reaction-network components most likely to control the final prediction.[8] Yet industrial catalyst selection occurs after intrinsic performance has been transformed by catalyst inventory, reactor design, operating conditions, recycle, separations and capital and operating costs. The comparative question is therefore broader than predictive accuracy at any one scale: does the ordering generated by an upstream screen survive this propagation?

This question is most consequential near the decision frontier. Screening is comparative, and a model may remain well correlated across a broad candidate population while changing the identity of the leading candidate. Global predictive quality therefore does not guarantee fidelity where experimental and computational resources are concentrated. The same issue arises under uncertainty: adaptive-sampling and Bayesian-error approaches have shown that uncertainty should be interpreted relative to the quantity or decision being resolved, rather than treated as a uniform reason to refine every part of a model.[9,10]

We formulate this problem in two directions. Forward propagation maps catalyst properties through kinetics and process optimization to an industrial objective. Backward design starts from an economic target, identifies the catalyst-property improvement required to reach it, and tests whether that target lies within an admissible catalyst-property manifold. The resulting framework separates rank change, its process mechanism and the physical reachability of the implied catalyst target.

We apply the framework to two reaction systems and one rank-preservation control. Ammonia synthesis probes an activity-inventory/reactor-demand pathway across a 15-metal screen grounded in established scaling, Brønsted-Evans-Polanyi and volcano relations for transition-metal ammonia synthesis.[5,11,12] CO2-to-methanol probes a selectivity-recycle pathway using literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states together with an explicit process loop; this reaction class is known to be strongly conditioned by conversion, selectivity, recycle and separation choices at process scale.[13–15] Au/TiO2 CO oxidation provides a counterfactual with a literature-calibrated monotonic size-activity mapping and a long-established sensitivity of low-temperature CO oxidation to supported Au particle size.[16–19]

We then embed the same decision chain in a frozen scientific-action environment. Tool-using language-model agents, autonomous chemical platforms and Bayesian experiment-selection methods have shown that scientific workflows can be organized around iterative action selection rather than one-shot prediction.[20–27] Recent catalyst-design and autonomous-catalysis frameworks extend this idea toward literature-guided optimization, reaction-network construction, tool orchestration and verification-aware scientific workflows.[28–31] Here the agent does not generate physical results. It allocates a finite compute budget across fixed scientific actions, while the underlying physics and economics remain deterministic.

Across the three catalytic systems, multiscale propagation spans a continuum from exact rank preservation to local reshuffling and decision-frontier inversion. The governing variable is the strength and topology of catalyst-specific coupling to downstream process decisions. The agent benchmark extends the same principle to computation: additional compute is valuable only when it resolves a part of the chain that can still change the decision.

## Results

### Industrial propagation reverses the ammonia decision frontier

The 15-metal ammonia screen places Ru, Os and Fe first, second and third in atomic activity, consistent with the established activity landscape for transition-metal ammonia synthesis.[11,12] After candidate-specific process optimization and evaluation of the catalyst-dependent economic objective, the order becomes Fe, Ru and Os, with costs of 15.292, 22.031 and 25.832 US dollars per tonne of NH3, respectively (Fig. 1a).

The reversal is concentrated at the decision frontier. Spearman correlation is -0.50 for the top three candidates but 0.929 across all 15 metals (Fig. 1b). The screen therefore remains globally informative while changing the preferred candidate among the materials most likely to be pursued. This separation between global rank agreement and frontier fidelity is central to the downstream decision.

Candidate-specific process freedom does not remove the inversion. With pressure-dependent equipment capital included, Fe occupies an interior optimum near 425 °C and 180 bar, Ru an interior high-pressure optimum near 450 °C and 425 bar, and Os a broad shallow high-pressure minimum (Fig. 2a). Their preferred operating regimes differ, yet the economic ordering remains Fe > Ru > Os.

Uncertainty is filtered by the same process structure. Across 1,000 descriptor-uncertainty draws, Fe is feasible under the specified bed-volume criterion in 79.9% of realizations, remains top-ranked in 28.2%, and remains within the actionable top three in 94.0% (Fig. 2b). Energetic uncertainty can therefore be amplified by kinetics, truncated by a feasibility boundary or absorbed by process reoptimization before it reaches the final decision. This interpretation is consistent with ensemble-based error estimation and uncertainty-guided model refinement, where uncertainty is propagated to the observable of interest rather than treated solely at the electronic-structure level.[8–10]

### Backward design separates economic requirement from physical reachability

Forward propagation identifies the preferred catalyst but does not reveal how far a losing candidate lies from parity. We therefore increased Ru activity and reoptimized the full process at each multiplier. Ru reaches the Fe economic reference only after an approximately 201-fold activity increase (Fig. 3a).

The corresponding scaling-consistent design space is much narrower. Accessible activity headroom is 1.090-fold at 673 K and reaches a maximum of 2.525-fold over the frozen process-state library. Under strict scaling, the minimum Ru cost remains 21.398 US dollars per tonne of NH3 at E_N = -1.215 eV, above Fe (Fig. 3b). The activity-only route to parity therefore lies far outside the current scaling-consistent manifold. This comparison is anchored in the same class of adsorption-energy scaling and BEP relations that make descriptor-based catalyst maps possible in the first place.[4,5]

Backward design thus converts an economic disadvantage into a quantitative catalyst target and immediately tests whether that target is compatible with the material class represented by the scaling relation. For the present ammonia system, incremental intrinsic-activity improvement cannot close the modeled economic gap.

### Methanol ranking reshaping follows a selectivity-recycle pathway

The same framework was transferred to a distinct process architecture using four literature-derived Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states.[13] With space-time yield per gram of Re as the upstream metric, the order is 1 wt% Re at 250 °C, 1 wt% Re at 200 °C, 5 wt% Re at 200 °C and 5 wt% Re at 250 °C. After propagation through the explicit recycle and separation model at the source-anchored 2% purge condition, the economic order becomes 5 wt% Re at 200 °C, 1 wt% Re at 200 °C, 1 wt% Re at 250 °C and 5 wt% Re at 250 °C, with net production costs of 943, 967, 975 and 1,258 euros per tonne, respectively (Fig. 4a).

The Re-normalized upstream winner falls from first to third. Across the four states, Spearman rho is 0.20, Kendall tau is 0.00 and three of six pairwise comparisons invert. The mechanism differs from ammonia. Methane formation consumes reactants and increases gas accumulation, purge, recycle and compression burden. At the 5 wt% Re, 250 °C benchmark, local economic leverage is 0.37579 for methane suppression, 0.05883 for single-pass conversion and 0.00289 for space-time yield (Fig. 4b). The process sensitivity is consistent with the broader CO2-to-methanol literature, in which equilibrium-limited conversion, recycle architecture and separation burden materially affect plant-level performance.[14,15]

The reshuffling persists across the available loop degree of freedom. Sweeping purge from 0.5% to 40% over 396 levels leaves the Re-normalized upstream winner economically suboptimal at every purge fraction; rho never exceeds 0.40 and at least two of six pairs remain inverted. Candidate-specific purge optimization changes the detailed order but does not restore the upstream ranking.

The screening denominator determines which state appears to fail at the frontier. Space-time yield per gram of Re places 1 wt% Re at 250 °C first and produces a top-rank reversal. Single-pass methanol yield and space-time yield per gram of total catalyst instead place 5 wt% Re at 200 °C first, coinciding with the economic winner, while the global rank statistics remain rho = 0.20, tau = 0.00 and three of six inverted pairs. The process reshapes the ordering under all three admissible metrics; the chosen upstream objective determines the identity of the apparent winner.

### Coupling topology determines whether rankings invert or survive

Ammonia and methanol expose two distinct routes from catalyst properties to process cost (Fig. 5a). In ammonia, activity primarily controls catalyst inventory, reactor demand and operating severity. In methanol, selectivity controls reactant loss, gas accumulation, purge, recycle and compression. In both cases, candidate-specific downstream coupling is strong enough to alter relative burden near the decision frontier.

A literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 CO-oxidation control tests the complementary regime. Low-temperature CO oxidation on supported Au has long shown pronounced particle-size and support effects,[16,17] and the present control uses an absolute-rate anchor from Janssens et al.[18] together with the closest-loading size dependence from Overbury et al.[19] Candidate states differ only in Au particle size from 2 to 6 nm under a common operating condition and process topology. Under this mapping, intrinsic activity and required catalyst burden have the same order, 2 > 3 > 4 > 5 > 6 nm. Spearman rho and Kendall tau are both 1.000, no pairwise comparison inverts, and all 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws preserve the complete order (Fig. 5b).

A semi-open extension allows candidate-specific kinetic perturbations and optimization over literature-constrained temperature and feed-ratio windows. Exact ordering is preserved in 92.16% of 10,000 draws over 273.15–293.15 K, with mean rho = 0.99214. Extending the window to 313.15 K lowers exact preservation to 72.62% while retaining mean rho = 0.96802. Moderate downstream freedom therefore introduces local reshuffling without erasing the upstream structure.

The three systems define a continuum. A common monotonic mapping preserves the screen; moderate candidate-specific freedom weakens exact preservation; strong coupling to downstream process variables can alter the industrial frontier. The relevant object is therefore the coupling topology between catalyst properties, screening objectives and downstream decision variables.

### Decision-aware compute allocation has a bounded operating envelope

We next asked whether computation itself could be allocated according to decision value. DISCOVER V1 exposes a frozen scientific-action interface over the same multiscale decision chain. The agent selects admissible actions under a finite compute budget; all numerical physics and economics are executed by fixed tools. The primary endpoint requires the correct economic winner, decision-pair ordering and reachability classification. Numerical recovery of the backward parity multiplier is scored separately.

This setup sits at the intersection of tool-using language models and adaptive scientific experimentation. ReAct formalized iterative reasoning with external actions,[20] while ChemCrow and Coscientist demonstrated tool-mediated chemistry planning and execution.[21,22] Autonomous laboratories and Bayesian reaction-optimization systems have independently shown the value of selecting the next experiment or condition from the evolving state of a scientific search.[23–27] More recent catalyst-focused workflows increasingly combine literature extraction, optimization, reaction-network generation and verification-aware orchestration.[28–31]

On the anonymous closed-book benchmark, complete-decision recovery is 6/35 for the nano tier, 15/35 for the mini tier and 35/35 for the strong tier (Fig. 6a). The deterministic fixed policy completes at 206 compute units (CU). Around this boundary, the strong model reaches 20/20 complete decisions at 75 CU, with a median 52.5 CU to the ledger-true decision-stable point. At 50 CU, completion falls to 13/20 even though the winner and decision pair remain correct in all 20 runs; the closing reachability action becomes unaffordable in the remaining cases.

Decision convergence precedes quantitative-target convergence. Under the frozen first-record convention, the canonical Ru-to-Fe parity multiplier is recovered in 9/20 runs at 75 CU and 20/20 at 225 CU. Thus 75 CU is the lowest tested stable complete-decision budget, whereas 225 CU is the lowest tested budget with 20/20 canonical backward-target recovery (Fig. 6b). The industrial decision can close before every intermediate numerical quantity converges, provided that the residual quantitative error does not cross the reachability boundary.

The below-threshold mechanism is adaptive scope allocation. From 50 to 175 CU, all strong-model runs construct a process window smaller than the full 14,136-state domain and subsequently use it in a scoped action. Canonical narrow-window allocation falls to 1/8 at 200 CU and disappears at 225 and 250 CU. Neither weaker tier reproduces this behavior at 175 CU. Providing the mini tier with typed, prevalidated tool parameters removes undeclared-argument errors and improves winner identification, but complete decisions remain 0/20 at 175 CU. Increasing its budget to 225, 300 and 400 CU yields 6/20, 4/20 and 7/20 complete decisions, respectively.

The upper edge appears when the budget ceases to constrain the policy. Under a non-binding 5,000-CU allowance, all 20 strong runs reach the same audited decision, but median final spend rises to 714 CU and median post-stability spend is 148 CU. Adaptive allocation therefore has its clearest value in the constrained regime where selective computation closes a decision that deterministic coverage cannot yet afford. The operating envelope is jointly set by model capability, budget and the unresolved state of the decision chain.

## Discussion

These results place catalyst screening at the industrial decision frontier. Activity, selectivity and adsorption-derived descriptors remain essential scientific variables, but their decision value depends on the downstream process pathways they control. Descriptor theory, scaling relations and microkinetic modelling provide a powerful basis for compressing catalytic complexity,[1,4–8] yet a large intrinsic change can have little economic leverage if process constraints absorb it. A smaller perturbation can dominate when it changes feasibility, recycle, separation or equipment burden. Screening objectives should therefore be selected with explicit knowledge of the catalyst-to-process coupling they are intended to probe.

Global rank correlation alone is insufficient for this purpose. In ammonia, the full 15-metal ordering remains strongly correlated while the top-three order changes. A screening model can therefore look successful over the population yet direct effort toward a different leading candidate. Frontier-local rank fidelity, pairwise decision changes and winner identity provide more direct measures of whether a screen supports the intended decision.

Backward design adds a complementary constraint. Industrial economics can define a quantitative catalyst target, but that target becomes scientifically actionable only after comparison with the reachable material-property manifold. The approximately 201-fold Ru requirement and 2.525-fold maximum scaling-consistent headroom illustrate the separation. Such a gap points away from incremental activity optimization and toward changes in selectivity, stability, process architecture or material class that can alter the governing downstream pathway.

The cross-reaction comparison further argues against universal screening heuristics. Ammonia is governed here by an activity-inventory/reactor-demand route; methanol by selectivity-sensitive recycle economics. The appropriate upstream metric therefore depends on process architecture and on how catalytic performance is normalized. Transfer across reactions should begin by identifying the relevant coupling topology, then choosing descriptors and experiments that interrogate that topology.

The Au/TiO2 control provides the counterfactual required to interpret these inversions. Multiscale propagation preserves an upstream ranking when candidates enter a common monotonic downstream mapping, and moderate operating freedom can introduce local reorderings while leaving the global structure strongly correlated. Explicit multiscale decision analysis becomes most important when candidate-specific downstream coupling near the frontier is strong enough to compete with upstream performance differences.

The agent benchmark extends the same principle to scientific computation. Active learning and Bayesian optimization have long framed experiment selection as a choice about where the next unit of information is most valuable,[9,25] and autonomous chemistry platforms increasingly make that choice inside executable workflows.[21–24,26–31] Here the relevant utility is narrower: compute has decision value when it resolves an uncertainty that can still change the final choice. Under a binding budget, the strong model reduces process scope and redirects resources toward unresolved backward-design and reachability links; when the budget is loose, that advantage disappears and spending can continue after the decision is already stable. Scientific agents should therefore be evaluated through capability- and budget-dependent operating envelopes rather than a single efficiency number.

Together, forward propagation, backward design and decision-aware compute allocation form a single decision-centered workflow. Forward analysis determines whether an upstream ranking survives industrial constraints. Backward analysis converts the industrial gap into a catalyst-property requirement and tests its reachability. Adaptive allocation identifies which remaining calculation is worth performing. The result is a framework for linking catalyst discovery to industrial choice without assuming that the best atomic-scale candidate is also the best process-level decision.

## Methods

### Multiscale decision analysis

Each candidate is propagated through a frozen chain linking catalyst-level inputs to a process-level economic objective. Rankings before and after propagation are compared using Spearman and Kendall correlations, pairwise inversions and top-rank identity. Frontier analyses focus on leading upstream candidates while full-set statistics distinguish local decision changes from global rank disorder. Backward design varies a selected catalyst property, reoptimizes the downstream process and identifies the value required for economic parity, which is then compared with the reachable catalyst-property or scaling manifold.

### Ammonia synthesis

The ammonia analysis uses NH3-FINAL-1.1. The 15-metal screen is propagated through frozen scaling and kinetic relations, catalyst productivity and inventory, candidate-specific process-state optimization, pressure-dependent equipment capital and the seven catalyst-dependent economic cost pools. Temperature, pressure and separator temperature are reoptimized per candidate. Uncertainty is propagated through 1,000 descriptor draws; Fe feasibility is evaluated against the 90 m3 bed-volume criterion. The Ru backward-design calculation varies activity with full downstream reoptimization, and reachability is evaluated over the frozen descriptor path and process-state library. The mechanistic and scaling basis follows the established transition-metal ammonia-synthesis literature.[5,11,12]

### CO2-to-methanol

The methanol analysis uses the restored D01 v3 recycle/separation model and four Re/TiO2 states derived from the 1 and 5 wt% catalysts reported by Gothe et al.[13] The canonical comparison uses 2% purge. Upstream rankings are reconstructed from space-time yield per gram of Re, single-pass methanol yield and space-time yield per gram of catalyst; downstream ranking uses the frozen near-full-plant net production cost. Re purchase price is excluded from this objective. Purge robustness spans 0.5–40% over 396 levels, with additional candidate-specific purge reoptimization. Local leverage for space-time yield, conversion and methane suppression is evaluated at the 5 wt% Re, 250 °C benchmark. The explicit-loop interpretation is consistent with established CO2-to-methanol process assessments in which recycle, compression and separation contribute materially to plant performance.[14,15]

### Au/TiO2 rank-preservation control

The canonical control compares 2–6 nm Au/TiO2 states under a common fixed condition and process topology. The reference rate, Au loading and dispersion are anchored to Janssens et al.,[18] and the nominal size exponent to the 4.5 wt% Au/TiO2 series of Overbury et al.[19] The broader physical basis for low-temperature supported-Au activity and size sensitivity follows the established Au/TiO2 literature.[16,17] Rank preservation is evaluated in 10,000 predefined literature-envelope draws. A semi-open extension perturbs candidate kinetics and independently optimizes temperature and O2/CO ratio within literature-constrained windows.

### Decision-aware agent benchmark

DISCOVER V1 exposes a frozen 11-action interface. The agent receives a finite CU budget and selects admissible actions; the environment executes all numerical work. The primary endpoint is `winner_correct ∧ pair_decision_correct ∧ reachability_correct`; backward parity-multiplier accuracy is scored independently. The deterministic fixed-policy reference completes at 206 CU. Cross-model tests use the frozen anonymous closed-book task. DISCOVER-BOUNDARY-C1 probes the strong tier around the fixed-policy boundary, the weaker-tier transfer cells and a non-binding 5,000-CU allowance. Narrow-window allocation requires a window smaller than the 14,136-state domain followed by a successful scoped action using that window. All Agent headline values are reproducible from retained raw traces with the frozen scorer; clean-checkout CI verifies source hashes and replays the scorer without invoking an LLM or generating new traces.

# NH3 Ru/Fe: primary-source scope audit for precious-metal contract structures

**Review date 2026-10-12.** Aim: substantiate only the *existence of types of precious-metal transactions*. Nothing below establishes the availability, commercial fee or reliability of a ruthenium ammonia-synthesis catalyst lease in a specific plant.

## Industry first-party materials (direct providers)

### BASF Environmental Catalyst and Metal Solutions (ECMS)

[Precious Metal Trading and Commercial Services](https://basf-catalystsmetals.com/en-us/precious-metals/trading)

The provider lists precious-metal **leases and consignments**, long-term supply agreements, spot and forward price arrangements and client-held **pool accounts**; explicitly says metal can be borrowed for catalyst production, use and recycling and that leases can reduce customer capital cost. Another [BASF ECMS precious metals overview](https://www.basf-catalystsmetals.com/en-us/precious-metals) explicitly includes **Ru** among its traded metals.

**Supported:** broad PGM leasing/consignment and Ru trading exist in the industry's provider portfolio.

**Not supported:** that BASF currently leases Ru for KAAP ammonia synthesis; any Ru-specific percentage, collateral, limit, catalyst return yield, guaranteed metal buyback or settled PGM metal spot price.

### Heraeus Precious Metals

[Trading overview](https://www.heraeus-precious-metals.com/en/precious-metal-trading/trading-overview/) explicitly includes buying/selling **ruthenium**, weight accounts, metal transfers and risk hedging. [Heraeus recycling of critical raw materials](https://www.heraeus-precious-metals.com/en/products-solutions/category/hydrogen-systems/recycling-solutions-for-a-circular-hydrogen-economy/) states that recovered precious metals (including Ru in the provider's hydrogen-sector portfolio) can be credited to a customer's metal pool account and then reused, sold or delivered.

**Supported:** recovered Ru can be handled through industrial recycling/trading and account-credit processes in other metal-bearing industries.

**Not supported:** Ru/C spent ammonia-catalyst refining yield, financial settlement period, a Ru-specific lease quote or automatic saleability of Ru salt at metallic-Ru value. A **client-owned weight account** is still an operator-owned Ru inventory for capital-financing analysis; an account credit is not automatically a zero-principal supplier consignment.

### Johnson Matthey

[Annual Report & Accounts 2020](https://matthey.com/documents/161599/162491/Accounts.pdf/2c2ef1ed-3f49-0e1a-a002-b505b0a9c7bf): JM itself historically leased precious metal from banks over periods typically measured in months, incurring fees, to finance temporary metal requirements. [JM PGM Services sector transcript (2019)](https://matthey.com/documents/161599/163511/Sector-call-PGMS-transcript.pdf/d166bdcf-c72f-95e7-629d-eccd88b9cdda) describes customer-account metal, return timing and the working-capital impact of leased metal.

**Supported:** metal leasing moves expense from metal principal/inventory ownership to recurring contract fees plus metal return obligations.

**Not supported:** a quoted Ru/C ammonia catalyst fee, or even that the short-term banks' practice maps onto a guaranteed multi-decade ammonia plant lease. Our 25-year fee scenarios are deliberately *not* calibrated from this literature.

## Economic model classification and missing contract fields

| Model class | Operator owns Ru metal? | Operator pays for Ru metal principal? | Additional cost / credit events | Open industrial evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Own and recover | Yes | Yes, at catalyst commissioning/replacement | Refined Ru proceeds at return, processing fee, sale delay | Actual recovery-to-metal yield, buyback agreement, residual metal value |
| Own with guaranteed buyback | Yes | Yes | Guaranteed buyback floor at return | Existence/level of an ammonia Ru/C guaranteed buyback floor |
| Supplier metal lease | No (by model assumption) | No | Year-end metal-rent payment, certified fine-metal deficit charge, processing fee, collateral deposit/refund | Ru-specific contract, percentage, title/insurance, fine-metal return penalty |
| Supplier consignment | No (by model assumption) | No | Per-loading service fee, possible refundable security and loss make-up | Whether consignment is offered for Ru/C ammonia, title, monthly finance costs |
| Client's metal pool account | **Yes** (client account) | **Yes** or preexisting metal supplied | Trading fees, in/out transfers, refined metal recredits | Title and financing; do NOT classify as supplier metal by default |

## Model parameter status (all non-source-calibrated)

Annual lease charges 0–12%, consignment cycle fees 0–4%, security collateral 25%, refundable fraction 100%, metal value shortfall charged at 100%, refining yield 95%, $500/kg processing, half-year shortfall-bill delay, and hypothetical 80–100% buyback payout floors are **stress-test numbers invented solely to define a transparent decision boundary**. Sources above do not provide these terms. A zero lease/consignment fee is a mathematical bound, not an offered transaction. Keep outputs out of the canonical manuscript until actual contract evidence and engineering Ru/Fe feasibility checks are obtained.

## Required for actual industrial validity

Obtain written supplier contract terms for who holds legal title to Ru metal throughout use and reclamation, rental invoices or finance charges per time, deposits/margin calls, certified fine-metal return and settlement requirements, refinery assay/recovery mass-balance, additional fees, replacement intervals, and buyback purchase order terms. Only then calibrate an industrial DCF comparison. Evaluate Fe's actual KAAP-type low-pressure reactor feasibility separately.

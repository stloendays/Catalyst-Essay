## Figure 1 | A globally correlated catalyst screen can invert at the industrial decision frontier

**a,** Atomic-to-economic ranking propagation for the 15-metal ammonia screen. Intrinsic activity ranks **Ru > Os > Fe**, whereas candidate-specific process optimization and the frozen catalyst-dependent economic objective rank **Fe > Ru > Os**, with costs of **15.292, 22.031 and 25.832 US dollars per tonne of NH3**, respectively. **b,** Rolling Top-K rank fidelity between the upstream activity order and the downstream economic order. The leading three candidates give **Spearman rho = -0.50** and **Kendall tau = -0.33**, while the full 15-metal screen retains a raw **Spearman rho = 0.929**. The ranking conflict is therefore concentrated at the decision frontier rather than distributed across the complete screen.

## Figure 2 | Metal cost and process reoptimization jointly define the ammonia decision boundary

**a,** Candidate-specific operating regimes after process and economic reoptimization. Fe occupies an interior optimum near **425 °C / 180 bar / 30 °C separator**, Ru near **450 °C / 425 bar / 25 °C**, and Os retains a broad shallow high-pressure minimum. **b,** Canonical Fe-Ru cost decomposition and equal-price causal intervention. The canonical Ru-Fe gap is **6.739 US dollars per tonne of NH3**, with fresh-feed compression (**+4.632**), metal inventory (**+1.763**), compressor capital (**+1.181**) and refrigeration (**+0.629**) partly offset by vessel pressure, recycle compression and reactor-base terms. Setting the Ru metal price equal to Fe = **8 US dollars per kilogram** and fully reoptimizing all **14,136** process states shifts Ru to **425 °C / 170 bar / 30 °C** and lowers its cost to **14.712 US dollars per tonne**, **0.580 US dollars per tonne below Fe**. **c,** Decision-level outcomes across the frozen 1,000-draw descriptor Monte Carlo: Fe feasibility **79.9%**, Fe economic Top-1 probability **68.1% (681/1000)**, atomic-to-economic Top-1 survival **28.2%**, and Fe Top-3 actionable probability **94.0%**. **d,** Local economic robustness around the canonical regime. In the preregistered 5,000-draw joint cost-parameter Monte Carlo, Fe remains lower-cost in **5,000/5,000** draws, with a minimum sampled Ru-Fe gap of **2.382 US dollars per tonne**. The corresponding Ru activity-parity distribution has **p05 = 70.78-fold, median = 174.27-fold and p95 = 462.00-fold**, with the canonical **201.22-fold** value shown as reference. The equal-price intervention is the causal-boundary test; the Monte Carlo is the local-robustness test.

## Figure 3 | Backward design separates the catalyst improvement required by economics from the improvement reachable on the current material manifold

**a,** Ru activity-only backward sweep with full process reoptimization. Under the canonical economic parameters, Ru reaches the Fe cost reference only after a **201.22-fold** intrinsic-activity increase. **b,** Scaling-consistent reachability. Activity headroom is **1.090-fold at 673 K** and reaches a maximum of **2.525-fold** over the frozen process-state library; the lowest strict-scaling Ru cost is **21.398 US dollars per tonne of NH3 at E_N = -1.215 eV**, above Fe. The canonical 201.22-fold requirement is one economic reference; even the **70.78-fold** 5th-percentile target from the joint cost analysis remains far above the maximum scaling-consistent headroom.

## Figure 4 | Methanol catalyst-state rankings reshape through a selectivity-recycle pathway and remain stable to tested economic uncertainty

**a,** Upstream-to-economic ranking propagation for four Re/TiO2 catalyst-temperature states. Using space-time yield per gram of Re, the upstream order is **1 wt% Re / 250 °C > 1 wt% Re / 200 °C > 5 wt% Re / 200 °C > 5 wt% Re / 250 °C**, whereas the canonical 2% purge economic order is **5 wt% Re / 200 °C > 1 wt% Re / 200 °C > 1 wt% Re / 250 °C > 5 wt% Re / 250 °C**. The comparison gives **Spearman rho = 0.20**, **Kendall tau = 0.00** and **3/6 pairwise inversions**. **b,** Purge robustness and local economic leverage. Across **0.5-40%** purge over **396 levels**, the Re-normalized upstream winner remains economically suboptimal, rho never exceeds **0.40**, and at least **2/6** pairs remain inverted. At 5 wt% Re / 250 °C, local leverage is **0.00289** for space-time yield, **0.05883** for single-pass conversion and **0.37579** for methane suppression. **c,** Candidate-by-rank probability under the 5,000-draw economic-parameter analysis. The canonical D01 order is retained in **5,000/5,000** draws. Because Re purchase and replacement are excluded from the canonical D01 objective, sampled metal price and lifetime are structurally inactive in that calculation; a separately labelled active-Re replacement extension also preserves the same order in **5,000/5,000** draws.

## Figure 5 | Catalyst-to-process coupling topology determines whether an upstream ranking reshapes or survives

**a,** Reaction-specific catalyst-to-process pathways. In NH3, **intrinsic activity + metal cost** control catalyst inventory and the preferred operating regime, which redistribute compression, reactor and equipment burden before the economic ranking is evaluated. In CO2-to-methanol, **selectivity** controls reactant loss and gas accumulation, which propagate through purge, recycle and compression. The reactions are evaluated against their own frozen downstream economic objectives; absolute cost values are not compared across reactions. **b,** Literature-calibrated Au/TiO2 CO-oxidation rank-preservation control. Under a common process mapping, intrinsic activity and required catalyst burden preserve the order **2 > 3 > 4 > 5 > 6 nm**, with **Spearman rho = 1.000**, **Kendall tau = 1.000**, zero pairwise inversions and full preservation in **10,000/10,000** predefined literature-envelope draws. A semi-open 273.15-293.15 K extension retains the exact order in **92.16%** of draws with mean rho **0.99214**. The control shows that multiscale propagation does not generate inversion by construction.

## Figure 6 | Decision-aware agents make the multiscale decision framework repeatedly executable

All panels use the frozen DISCOVER V1 environment and DISCOVER-BOUNDARY-C1 budget series. The language-model policy selects admissible actions, while all numerical physics and economics are executed by deterministic tools. **a,** Complete-decision recovery versus compute budget. The deterministic fixed policy completes at **206 CU**, whereas the strong tier reaches the lowest tested stable complete-decision allowance at **75 CU**. The protocol-complete S1-S3 oracle lower bound is **22 CU**, so 75 CU is **3.41 times** oracle; the median decision-stable spend in the 75-CU cell is **52.5 CU**, or **2.39 times** oracle. **b,** Scoped process search as the below-threshold mechanism. Narrow-window allocation appears in the constrained regime and falls to **0/20** at 225 CU and under the non-binding 5,000-CU allowance. **c,** Upper compute boundary. Under the non-binding allowance, median decision stability shifts to **566 CU**, followed by a median **148 CU** of additional spend before self-stop, for **714 CU** median final spend. Budget pressure therefore activates search compression. The broader role of this layer is workflow scalability: reaction-specific deterministic modules retain scientific truth, while the Agent supplies reusable action orchestration so the same ranking–parity–reachability logic can be executed repeatedly across decision instances instead of being manually scripted case by case.
